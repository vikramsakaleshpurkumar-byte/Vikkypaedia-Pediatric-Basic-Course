# Chapter 402: Morphogenesis of the Liver and Biliary System
*Stacey S. Huppert and William F. Balistreri*

## OVERVIEW AND EPIDEMIOLOGY
**Morphogenesis of the liver and biliary system** begins in the 3rd–4th week of gestation as an endodermal outgrowth (**hepatic diverticulum**) from the ventral foregut just cranial to the yolk sac junction, induced by **fibroblast growth factors (FGFs)** from the cardiogenic mesoderm and **bone morphogenetic proteins (BMPs)** from the septum transversum mesenchyme.

## CLINICAL MANIFESTATIONS AND PATHOPHYSIOLOGY
The cranial pars hepatica proliferates into hepatoblasts (bipotential precursors expressing *HNF4A*, *FOXA2*, and *PROX1* that differentiate into hepatocytes and cholangiocytes), while the caudal pars cystica forms the gallbladder and cystic duct. Intrahepatic bile ducts develop from the **ductal plate**—a double-layered cylindrical sleeve of hepatoblasts surrounding portal vein branches that undergoes remodeling regulated by the **Jagged1 (*JAG1*)–Notch2 (*NOTCH2*)** signaling pathway and *HNF1B*. Arrest or maldevelopment of ductal plate remodeling (**ductal plate malformation**) underlies **Alagille syndrome** (*JAG1* [118450], *NOTCH2* [610205]), **congenital hepatic fibrosis**, **Caroli disease/syndrome** (*PKHD1* [263200]), and biliary atresia splenic malformation (BASM) syndrome.

## DIAGNOSIS AND DIFFERENTIAL EVALUATION
Histopathologic examination of liver biopsy specimens demonstrates characteristic ductal plate configurations (persisting embryonic bile duct structures at the limiting plate with fibrous expansion in Caroli syndrome and ARPKD, versus bile duct paucity [bile duct-to-portal tract ratio <0.5] in Alagille syndrome). Genetic panels identify pathogenic variants in *JAG1*, *NOTCH2*, *PKHD1*, *HNF1B*, and *CFC1*.

#### **Table 402.1** Key Clinical, Diagnostic, and Therapeutic Summary for Morphogenesis of the Liver and Biliary System
| Clinical Entity / Parameter | Pathophysiology, Diagnosis & Management |
| --- | --- |
| **Hepatic Diverticulum Specification (Weeks 3–4)** | Endodermal ventral foregut induced by cardiac FGF2 and septum transversum BMP4; regulated by FOXA1/2, GATA4/6, PROX1 |
| **Ductal Plate Remodeling (Weeks 8–32)** | JAG1/NOTCH2, SOX9, HNF6, and HNF1B drive periportal hepatoblasts into polarized intrahepatic bile ducts |
| **Alagille Syndrome (OMIM #118450)** | JAG1 (94%) or NOTCH2 (2–4%) variants; bile duct paucity, butterfly vertebrae, posterior embryotoxon, peripheral pulmonary stenosis |
| **Ductal Plate Malformation / Ciliopathies** | PKHD1 (ARPKD / Caroli syndrome); fibrocystin defect on primary cilia causing ectatic intrahepatic bile ducts and congenital hepatic fibrosis |

## TREATMENT AND PROGNOSIS
Management targets the specific developmental ciliopathy or cholangiopathy: ursodeoxycholic acid (UDCA 15–20 mg/kg/day), ileal bile acid transporter (IBAT) inhibitors (**maralixibat**, **odevixibat**) for refractory cholestatic pruritus in Alagille syndrome, fat-soluble vitamin (A, D, E, K) supplementation, surveillance for portal hypertension in congenital hepatic fibrosis, and liver transplantation for decompensated biliary cirrhosis.
