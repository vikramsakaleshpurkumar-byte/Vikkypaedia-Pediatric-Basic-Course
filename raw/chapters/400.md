# Chapter 400: Pancreatic Fluid Collections
*Michael Wilschanski and Steven L. Werlin*

## OVERVIEW AND EPIDEMIOLOGY
**Pancreatic fluid collections (PFCs)** in children develop secondary to acute pancreatitis, pancreatic trauma (especially handlebar bicycle injuries compressing the pancreatic body against the vertebral column), ductal disruption, or hereditary pancreatitis (*PRSS1*, *SPINK1*, *CFTR*, *CTRC* variants). Under the Revised Atlanta Classification, collections are categorized by age (<4 weeks vs ≥4 weeks) and the presence of solid necrosis.

## CLINICAL MANIFESTATIONS AND PATHOPHYSIOLOGY
Acute peripancreatic fluid collections (APFCs) occur within 4 weeks of interstitial edematous pancreatitis and lack a defined wall; most resolve spontaneously without intervention. **Pancreatic pseudocysts** mature after ≥4 weeks as encapsulated, enzyme-rich fluid collections bounded by fibrous and granulation tissue without an epithelial lining. Acute necrotic collections (ANCs) and **walled-off necrosis (WON)** arise after necrotizing pancreatitis. Children with persistent collections (>6 cm) present with epigastric pain, early satiety, gastric outlet or biliary obstruction, persistent hyperamylasemia/hyperlipasemia, fever, or secondary infection.

## DIAGNOSIS AND DIFFERENTIAL EVALUATION
**Transabdominal ultrasound** is the primary screening and serial monitoring modality in children. **Contrast-enhanced CT** or **MRCP (magnetic resonance cholangiopancreatography)** delineates cyst wall maturity, ductal communication, biliary dilatation, and solid necrotic debris. Endoscopic ultrasound (EUS) characterizes wall thickness, vascularity (ruling out pseudoaneurysm), and internal debris prior to drainage.

#### **Table 400.1** Key Clinical, Diagnostic, and Therapeutic Summary for Pancreatic Fluid Collections
| Clinical Entity / Parameter | Pathophysiology, Diagnosis & Management |
| --- | --- |
| **Acute Peripancreatic Fluid Collection (APFC)** | <4 weeks after interstitial pancreatitis; homogeneous fluid without a defined wall; >80% resolve spontaneously with supportive care |
| **Pancreatic Pseudocyst** | ≥4 weeks; well-circumscribed encapsulated fluid collection rich in amylase/lipase without solid necrosis; EUS-guided cystogastrostomy if symptomatic |
| **Acute Necrotic Collection (ANC)** | <4 weeks after necrotizing pancreatitis; heterogeneous fluid and necrotic parenchyma; avoid early intervention unless infected |
| **Walled-Off Necrosis (WON)** | ≥4 weeks encapsulated pancreatic/peripancreatic necrosis; managed via step-up endoscopic transluminal drainage and necrosectomy |

## TREATMENT AND PROGNOSIS
Asymptomatic, uncomplicated pediatric pancreatic fluid collections and pseudocysts <5–6 cm are managed **conservatively** with enteral nutrition, analgesia, and serial ultrasonography, as >60–75% resolve spontaneously. Symptomatic, enlarging, infected, or complicated mature collections (≥4–6 weeks old) are treated primarily via **EUS-guided transmural endoscopic cystogastrostomy** using lumen-apposing metal stents (LAMS) or double-pigtail plastic stents. ERCP with transpapillary pancreatic duct stenting is indicated for main pancreatic duct disruption. Percutaneous catheter drainage or surgical cystenterostomy (Roux-en-Y cystojejunostomy) is reserved for collections inaccessible to endoscopy.
