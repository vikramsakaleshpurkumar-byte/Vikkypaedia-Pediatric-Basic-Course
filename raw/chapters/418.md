# Chapter 418: Ascites
*Jessica W. Wen and Chris A. Liacouras*

## OVERVIEW AND EPIDEMIOLOGY
**Ascites**—the pathologic accumulation of fluid within the peritoneal cavity—results in children primarily from chronic liver disease with portal hypertension, nephrotic syndrome, congestive heart failure / post-Fontan physiology, peritoneal serositis, or **lymphatic disruption (Subchapter 418.1: Chylous Ascites)**.

## CLINICAL MANIFESTATIONS AND PATHOPHYSIOLOGY
Hepatic ascites develops when **sinusoidal portal hypertension** and splanchnic arterial vasodilation (mediated by nitric oxide) decrease effective arterial blood volume, activating the **renin-angiotensin-aldosterone system (RAAS)**, sympathetic nervous system, and **antidiuretic hormone (ADH)** to cause avid renal sodium and water retention, compounded by **hypoalbuminemia** (reduced plasma oncotic pressure). Clinical signs include progressive abdominal girth, shifting dullness, fluid wave, scrotal/labial edema, umbilical or inguinal hernias, and respiratory compromise from diaphragmatic elevation. **Subchapter 418.1: Chylous Ascites** is characterized by milky, triglyceride-rich peritoneal fluid resulting from **congenital lymphatic malformations** (the #1 cause in neonates and infants, including primary intestinal lymphangiectasia / Waldmann disease), **thoracic duct / cisterna chyli trauma** (retroperitoneal surgery, Fontan surgery, or **non-accidental trauma [child abuse]**), malrotation with midgut volvulus, or lymphoma.

### 418.1 Chylous Ascites

## DIAGNOSIS AND DIFFERENTIAL EVALUATION
**Abdominal ultrasound** detects as little as 50–100 mL of free peritoneal fluid. **Diagnostic abdominal paracentesis** is essential for new-onset ascites or any child with ascites and fever/abdominal pain: (1) **Serum-to-Ascites Albumin Gradient (SAAG)** = `Serum Albumin − Ascitic Fluid Albumin`: **SAAG ≥1.1 g/dL** indicates **portal hypertension** (cirrhosis, Budd-Chiari syndrome, heart failure) with >97% accuracy, whereas **SAAG <1.1 g/dL** indicates non-portal hypertension etiologies (peritoneal carcinomatosis, tuberculosis, pancreatic ascites, serositis, or nephrotic syndrome); (2) **Ascitic fluid absolute polymorphonuclear (PMN) count ≥250 cells/mm³** diagnoses spontaneous bacterial peritonitis; and (3) **Chylous ascites** shows milky fluid with **triglycerides >200 mg/dL (often >1,000 mg/dL)**, lymphocyte predominance (>80% of WBCs), and chylomicrons.

#### **Table 418.1** Key Clinical, Diagnostic, and Therapeutic Summary for Ascites
| Clinical Entity / Parameter | Pathophysiology, Diagnosis & Management |
| --- | --- |
| **SAAG ≥ 1.1 g/dL (Portal Hypertension)** | Cirrhosis, biliary atresia, Budd-Chiari syndrome, post-Fontan / right heart failure; treat with Na restriction + Spironolactone ± Furosemide |
| **SAAG < 1.1 g/dL (Non-Portal Hypertension)** | Pancreatic ascites (high ascitic amylase), tuberculous peritonitis, peritoneal malignancy, lupus serositis |
| **Chylous Ascites (Subchapter 418.1)** | Milky fluid, triglycerides >200 mg/dL, lymphocyte predominance; congenital lymphangiectasia (#1 in infants) or surgical/abusive trauma; treat with MCT-based diet ± Octreotide/TPN |
| **Tense Ascites Paracentesis Protocol** | Therapeutic paracentesis with concurrent IV 25% Albumin infusion (0.5–1 g/kg) to prevent intravascular volume collapse and hepatorenal syndrome |

## TREATMENT AND PROGNOSIS
**Portal hypertensive ascites** is managed with **dietary sodium restriction (1–2 mEq Na+/kg/day)** and aldosterone antagonism with **spironolactone (2–3 mg/kg/day, up to 4–6 mg/kg/day)** ± **furosemide (1–2 mg/kg/day)** to maintain a 2.5:1 spironolactone-to-furosemide ratio and preserve normokalemia. Tense ascites causing respiratory distress requires **large-volume therapeutic paracentesis accompanied by intravenous 25% albumin (0.5–1.0 g/kg)** to prevent post-paracentesis circulatory dysfunction. **Chylous ascites** is treated with a **high-protein, low-long-chain-fat diet supplemented with medium-chain triglycerides (MCT oil/formula, e.g., Enfaport, Monogen)**—because MCTs are absorbed directly into the portal venous system bypassing intestinal lacteals—or **NPO + TPN and octreotide (somatostatin analog)** for 2–4 weeks in refractory cases.
