# Chapter 405: Metabolic Diseases of the Liver
*Julie Bonn and William F. Balistreri*

## OVERVIEW AND EPIDEMIOLOGY
**Metabolic diseases of the liver** encompass inherited disorders of bilirubin glucuronidation (**Subchapter 405.1**), copper transport (**Subchapter 405.2: Wilson Disease**), copper overload (**Subchapter 405.3: Indian Childhood Cirrhosis**), gestational alloimmune iron overload (**Subchapter 405.4: Neonatal Hemochromatosis / GALD**), and **alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency, cystic fibrosis liver disease, glycogen storage diseases, galactosemia, and hereditary tyrosinemia type 1** (**Subchapter 405.5**).

## CLINICAL MANIFESTATIONS AND PATHOPHYSIOLOGY
In **Subchapter 405.1**, *UGT1A1* variants cause **Crigler-Najjar syndrome type 1** (OMIM #218800; complete absence of UGT1A1 activity, unconjugated bilirubin 20–45 mg/dL, high risk of kernicterus, unresponsive to phenobarbital), **Crigler-Najjar type 2** (OMIM #606785; <10% activity, bilirubin 6–20 mg/dL, decreases ≥25% with phenobarbital), and **Gilbert syndrome** (OMIM #143500; benign *UGT1A1* TA7 promoter polymorphism causing mild unconjugated hyperbilirubinemia during fasting/illness). Conjugated hyperbilirubinemia with normal liver enzymes and normal GGT occurs in **Dubin-Johnson syndrome** (*ABCC2* / MRP2 defect [237500]; grossly **black liver**, elevated urinary coproporphyrin I >80%) and **Rotor syndrome** (*SLCO1B1* + *SLCO1B3* digenic defect [237450]; normal liver color). **Subchapter 405.2: Wilson Disease** (OMIM #277900, autosomal recessive *ATP7B* on 13q14.3) impairs copper incorporation into ceruloplasmin and biliary copper excretion, presenting after age 3–5 years with asymptomatic transaminasemia, chronic hepatitis, cirrhosis, **Coombs-negative hemolytic anemia**, **Kayser-Fleischer corneal rings**, and neuropsychiatric movement disorders (dysarthria, dystonia, wing-beating tremor). **Subchapter 405.4: Neonatal Hemochromatosis (Gestational Alloimmune Liver Disease [GALD])** is caused by maternal IgG antibodies activating fetal complement (C5b-9) on hepatocytes, presenting at birth with fetal hydrops, severe coagulopathy (INR >3–4), hypoglycemia, hypoalbuminemia, markedly elevated ferritin (>800–10,000 ng/mL), and **relatively normal or mildly elevated ALT/AST**.

## DIAGNOSIS AND DIFFERENTIAL EVALUATION
**Wilson disease** is diagnosed by **low serum ceruloplasmin (<20 mg/dL)**, **elevated 24-hour urinary copper (>40–100 µg/24 hr)**, slit-lamp examination for Kayser-Fleischer rings, **hepatic parenchymal copper >250 µg/g dry weight**, and biallelic *ATP7B* pathogenic variants (in fulminant Wilson disease, alkaline phosphatase-to-total bilirubin ratio is <4 and AST:ALT ratio is >2.2). **GALD / Neonatal hemochromatosis** is diagnosed by demonstrating **extrahepatic siderosis** (Prussian blue iron staining of minor salivary gland biopsy or T2-weighted MRI showing iron deposition in pancreas, heart, and thyroid with splenic sparing) and C5b-9 immunostaining on liver tissue.

## TREATMENT AND PROGNOSIS
**Crigler-Najjar type 1** requires intensive phototherapy, exchange transfusions, and liver transplantation (or *UGT1A1* gene therapy); **type 2** responds to **phenobarbital (3–5 mg/kg/day)**. **Wilson disease** is treated lifelong with copper chelators—**trientine** (20 mg/kg/day divided BID) or **D-penicillamine** (20 mg/kg/day + pyridoxine [Vitamin B6] 25 mg/day)—and/or **oral zinc** (which induces enterocyte metallothionein to block intestinal copper absorption); fulminant Wilson disease with hemolysis and renal failure requires urgent **liver transplantation**. **Neonatal hemochromatosis (GALD)** is treated immediately with **exchange transfusion + high-dose intravenous immunoglobulin (IVIG 1 g/kg)**, and recurrence in subsequent pregnancies (>80% risk) is prevented by administering **maternal IVIG (1 g/kg/week) starting at 14–18 weeks gestation**.
