## OVERVIEW AND EPIDEMIOLOGY
**Viral hepatitis** in infants, children, and adolescents is caused primarily by the five hepatotropic viruses—**Hepatitis A (HAV)**, **Hepatitis B (HBV)**, **Hepatitis C (HCV)**, **Hepatitis D (HDV)**, and **Hepatitis E (HEV)**—as well as systemic herpesviruses (**EBV, CMV, HSV, VZV, HHV-6**), adenovirus (notably type 41 with AAV2 co-infection), and enteroviruses.

## CLINICAL MANIFESTATIONS AND PATHOPHYSIOLOGY
**HAV** (*Picornaviridae*, non-enveloped RNA, fecal-oral transmission) is commonly asymptomatic/anicteric in children <5 years (>70% anicteric) and never causes chronic infection, though rare fulminant hepatitis can occur. **HBV** (*Hepadnaviridae*, partially double-stranded DNA virus) transmission in pediatrics is predominantly **perinatal/vertical** from HBsAg+/HBeAg+ mothers; chronicity is **inverse to age** (**90% chronicity in neonates**, 25–30% in children 1–5 years, <5% in adolescents/adults). Extrahepatic HBV manifestations include **Gianotti-Crosti syndrome** (papular acrodermatitis), membranous nephropathy, and polyarteritis nodosa. **HCV** (*Flaviviridae*, single-stranded RNA) is acquired vertically (~5–6% risk, doubled with maternal HIV co-infection) or via adolescent injection drug use; ~20–40% of vertically infected infants spontaneously clear HCV by age 2–3 years, while 60–80% develop chronic infection. **HDV** is a defective RNA virus requiring **HBsAg** for replication (co-infection or superinfection). **HEV** (*Hepeviridae*, fecal-oral waterborne) causes acute self-limited hepatitis but carries a 15–25% mortality rate in pregnant females.

## DIAGNOSIS AND DIFFERENTIAL EVALUATION
Acute **HAV** is diagnosed by **anti-HAV IgM**. **HBV** serology distinguishes acute infection (**HBsAg+, anti-HBc IgM+**), chronic infection (**HBsAg+ >6 months, anti-HBc IgG+**), immune-tolerant phase (HBeAg+, HBV DNA >10^7 IU/mL, normal ALT), immune-active phase (elevated ALT, elevated HBV DNA), window period (**anti-HBc IgM+ only**), and vaccine immunity (**anti-HBs+ only**). **HCV** is screened via **anti-HCV antibody at ≥18 months of age** (after maternal IgG clears) or earlier at **2–6 months via quantitative HCV RNA PCR**.

#### **Table 406.1** Key Clinical, Diagnostic, and Therapeutic Summary for Viral Hepatitis
| Clinical Entity / Parameter | Pathophysiology, Diagnosis & Management |
| --- | --- |
| **Hepatitis A Virus (HAV)** | Fecal-oral Picornavirus; anti-HAV IgM diagnostic; 0% chronicity; prevent with universal 2-dose HAV vaccine starting at 12–23 months |
| **Hepatitis B Virus (HBV) Perinatal Prophylaxis** | 90% neonatal chronicity without prophylaxis; give HBV vaccine + HBIG 0.5 mL IM within 12 hr of birth for HBsAg+ mothers; entecavir/tenofovir for chronic active HBV |
| **Hepatitis C Virus (HCV) in Children** | Vertical transmission ~5%; check HCV RNA PCR at 2–6 mo or anti-HCV ≥18 mo; cure at ≥3 yr with 8–12 wk pan-genotypic DAAs (glecaprevir/pibrentasvir or sofosbuvir/velpatasvir) |
| **Hepatitis D (HDV) & Hepatitis E (HEV)** | HDV requires HBsAg coat (prevented by HBV vaccination); HEV is fecal-oral waterborne virus causing severe fulminant hepatitis in pregnancy |

## TREATMENT AND PROGNOSIS
**Perinatal HBV prevention**: All infants born to **HBsAg-positive mothers** must receive **HBV vaccine AND Hepatitis B Immune Globulin (HBIG 0.5 mL IM) at separate anatomic sites within 12 hours of birth**, followed by completion of the vaccine series and post-vaccination serologic testing (HBsAg and anti-HBs) at 9–12 months of age. Chronic immune-active pediatric HBV is treated with **entecavir** (approved ≥2 years), **tenofovir disoproxil fumarate (TDF)** / **tenofovir alafenamide (TAF)**, or pegylated interferon-α2a. **Chronic pediatric HCV** in children **≥3 years of age** is cured (>98% sustained virologic response [SVR12]) with oral pan-genotypic **direct-acting antivirals (DAAs)**: **glecaprevir/pibrentasvir (Mavyret)** for 8 weeks or **sofosbuvir/velpatasvir (Epclusa)** for 12 weeks.
