# Chapter 415: Portal Hypertension and Varices
*Amy G. Feldman and Frederick J. Suchy*

## OVERVIEW AND EPIDEMIOLOGY
**Portal hypertension and varices** in children result from increased portal venous pressure (portal pressure >10 mm Hg or **hepatic venous pressure gradient [HVPG] >5 mm Hg**, with variceal bleeding risk when HVPG ≥10–12 mm Hg) due to prehepatic, intrahepatic, or posthepatic resistance to portal blood flow.

## CLINICAL MANIFESTATIONS AND PATHOPHYSIOLOGY
Unlike adults (in whom cirrhosis predominates), **extrahepatic portal vein obstruction (EHPVO / portal vein thrombosis with cavernous transformation)** is a leading cause of pediatric portal hypertension (30–50% of cases), triggered by **neonatal umbilical vein catheterization**, omphalitis, dehydration, or inherited thrombophilia (*JAK2*, Factor V Leiden, Protein C/S deficiency). **Intrahepatic causes** are subdivided into **presinusoidal** (**congenital hepatic fibrosis**, schistosomiasis), **sinusoidal** (**biliary atresia**, autoimmune hepatitis, Wilson disease, alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency, CFLD), and **postsinusoidal** (**sinusoidal obstruction syndrome [SOS/VOD]**). **Posthepatic causes** include **Budd-Chiari syndrome** (hepatic vein thrombosis) and constrictive pericarditis / post-Fontan physiology. Clinical hallmarks are **splenomegaly with hypersplenism (thrombocytopenia and leukopenia)**, **painless massive upper GI hemorrhage (hematemesis and melena) from gastroesophageal varices**, caput medusae, and ascites. Notice that children with **EHPVO or congenital hepatic fibrosis have normal hepatocellular synthetic function** (normal albumin, normal INR, no jaundice) and tolerate variceal bleeds far better than children with decompensated cirrhosis.

## DIAGNOSIS AND DIFFERENTIAL EVALUATION
**Doppler ultrasonography** of the portal, splenic, and hepatic veins is the essential first-line study: in **EHPVO**, it reveals replacement of the main portal vein by a tortuous network of collateral vessels (**cavernous transformation of the portal vein / portal cavernoma**) with normal liver echotexture and hepatopetal collateral flow. Upper GI endoscopy (**EGD**) is the gold standard for grading esophageal and gastric varices.

| Clinical Entity / Parameter | Pathophysiology, Diagnosis & Management |
| --- | --- |
| **Extrahepatic Portal Vein Obstruction (EHPVO)** | Prior neonatal umbilical vein catheterization/omphalitis; splenomegaly + variceal bleed with NORMAL liver synthetic function; 'cavernous transformation' on Doppler US; Meso-Rex bypass |

## TREATMENT AND PROGNOSIS
**Acute variceal hemorrhage** is a pediatric emergency managed via: (1) airway protection and **restrictive packed RBC transfusion (target hemoglobin 7–8 g/dL** — avoid overtransfusion, which rebounds portal pressure!), (2) immediate vasoactive splanchnic infusion with **octreotide (1–2 µg/kg IV bolus followed by 1–5 µg/kg/hr continuous infusion)**, (3) **prophylactic IV antibiotics (ceftriaxone)** to prevent bacterial translocation/SBP, and (4) urgent **endoscopic variceal ligation (EVL / banding)** (or endoscopic sclerotherapy in small infants <10 kg where banding caps cannot pass) within 12–24 hours. Refractory bleeding requires balloon tamponade (Sengstaken-Blakemore tube) as a bridge to **TIPS (transjugular intrahepatic portosystemic shunt)** or surgical shunting. Definitive surgical cure for **EHPVO** with a patent intrahepatic left portal vein is the **meso-Rex bypass shunt** (internal jugular vein graft from the superior mesenteric vein to the intrahepatic left portal vein in the Rex recess, which restores physiologic hepatopetal portal flow to the liver!).
