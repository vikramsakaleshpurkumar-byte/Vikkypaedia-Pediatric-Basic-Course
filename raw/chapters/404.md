## OVERVIEW AND EPIDEMIOLOGY
**Cholestasis**—defined as impaired canalicular bile flow resulting in accumulation of conjugated bilirubin, bile acids, and cholesterol in blood and extrahepatic tissues—is a time-critical pediatric emergency in neonates (**Subchapter 404.1: Neonatal Cholestasis**) and an important sign of genetic, autoimmune, or obstructive hepatobiliary disease in older children (**Subchapter 404.2: Cholestasis in the Older Child**).

## CLINICAL MANIFESTATIONS AND PATHOPHYSIOLOGY
Any infant with jaundice persisting beyond **2 weeks of age** must have fractionated serum bilirubin measured; **conjugated (direct) bilirubin >1.0 mg/dL** is always abnormal and never caused by physiologic jaundice or breast milk jaundice. **Biliary atresia (BA)** (1 in 8,000–15,000 live births; the #1 indication for pediatric liver transplantation) presents in thriving term neonates at 2–6 weeks of life with persistent jaundice, **acholic (pale/putty-colored) stools**, dark urine, and firm hepatomegaly; ~10–15% have **biliary atresia splenic malformation (BASM)** (polysplenia, situs inversus, preduodenal portal vein, interrupted IVC). Genetic intrahepatic cholestasis syndromes include **Alagille syndrome** (*JAG1* [118450]), **alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency** (*SERPINA1* PiZZ phenotype [613490]), **progressive familial intrahepatic cholestasis (PFIC1–6)** (*ATP8B1* [211600], *ABCB11* [601847], *ABCB4* [602347], *TJP2* [615878], *NR1H4* [617049], *MYO5B*), **citrin deficiency** (*SLC25A13*), galactosemia (*GALT*), and tyrosinemia type 1 (*FAH*).

## DIAGNOSIS AND DIFFERENTIAL EVALUATION
Urgent step-wise evaluation aims to identify treatable medical conditions (sepsis, UTI, galactosemia, tyrosinemia, hypothyroidism, panhypopituitarism, bile acid synthesis defects) and confirm or exclude **biliary atresia before 45–60 days of life**. Fasting abdominal ultrasound may show an absent/atretic gallbladder or the **triangular cord sign** at the porta hepatis and excludes choledochal cyst. Serum **MMP-7 (matrix metalloproteinase-7)** is markedly elevated in biliary atresia. **Percutaneous liver biopsy** in BA demonstrates **bile ductular proliferation, portal tract edema/fibrosis, and canalicular/ductular bile plugs**; the gold standard confirmation is **intraoperative cholangiography (IOC)** showing failure of contrast to pass through the extrahepatic biliary tree.

## TREATMENT AND PROGNOSIS
For confirmed **biliary atresia**, prompt **Kasai hepatoportoenterostomy (HPE)** before **60 days of age** restores bile drainage in >70–80% of infants (vs <25% when performed after 90 days). Post-Kasai care includes **ursodeoxycholic acid (UDCA 15–20 mg/kg/day)**, prophylactic trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole against ascending cholangitis, high-MCT calorie-dense formula (120–150 kcal/kg/day), and fat-soluble vitamins (**Vitamin A 5,000–15,000 IU/day, Vitamin D3 2,000–5,000 IU/day, TPGS-Vitamin E 15–25 IU/kg/day, Vitamin K1 2.5–5 mg/day**). Cholestatic pruritus is treated with UDCA, rifampin (5–10 mg/kg/day), cholestyramine, or IBAT inhibitors (**maralixibat**, **odevixibat**).
