## OVERVIEW AND EPIDEMIOLOGY
**Liver transplantation** has transformed survival for children with end-stage chronic liver disease, pediatric acute liver failure (PALF), non-resectable hepatic malignancies, and liver-based inborn errors of metabolism, achieving **1-year survival >93–95%** and **5-year survival >85–90%**.

## CLINICAL MANIFESTATIONS AND PATHOPHYSIOLOGY
**Biliary atresia** is the single most common indication for pediatric liver transplantation (~35–45% of all pediatric grafts), followed by **metabolic/genetic liver diseases (~20%)** (urea cycle defects [*OTC*, *CPS1*], maple syrup urine disease, Crigler-Najjar type 1, Wilson disease, alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency, PFIC, organic acidemias), **pediatric acute liver failure (~15%)**, **unresectable hepatoblastoma** (PRETEXT III/IV) (~8–10%), and autoimmune/cholestatic cirrhosis (Alagille syndrome, PSC). Pediatric organ allocation in the United States uses the **PELD (Pediatric End-Stage Liver Disease) score** for children **<12 years old** (calculated from **total bilirubin, INR, serum albumin, age <1 year, and growth failure [< −2 SD]**) and the **MELD-Na score** for adolescents **≥12 years old**.

## DIAGNOSIS AND DIFFERENTIAL EVALUATION
Pre-transplant evaluation optimizes nutritional status (MCT-enriched enteral or parenteral support, fat-soluble vitamins), completes **accelerated catch-up immunizations** (**live attenuated vaccines—MMR and varicella—MUST be administered ≥4 weeks BEFORE transplantation**; starting at ≥6 months of age in infants awaiting transplant), and maps vascular anatomy (confirming portal vein and IVC patency).

## TREATMENT AND PROGNOSIS
Surgical techniques tailored to small pediatric recipients include **whole-organ deceased donor**, **split-liver grafts** (left lateral segment [Segments II and III] for an infant and extended right lobe for an adult), and **living-donor left lateral segment transplantation**. Baseline maintenance immunosuppression relies on the calcineurin inhibitor **tacrolimus** (monitoring trough levels) + corticosteroids ± **mycophenolate mofetil**. Major post-transplant complications by timeline include: (1) **Early (<2 weeks)**: **hepatic artery thrombosis (HAT)** (the most dreaded early vascular complication in small infants, causing acute graft necrosis and biliary ischemia/leaks; screened by daily **Doppler ultrasound** for the first 7 days), primary non-function, and portal vein thrombosis; (2) **Intermediate**: acute T-cell-mediated cellular rejection (treated with pulse IV methylprednisolone), biliary strictures, and **CMV** or **EBV** viremia; and (3) **Late**: **EBV-driven Post-Transplant Lymphoproliferative Disorder (PTLD)** (highest risk in EBV-seronegative infants receiving an EBV-seropositive graft; treated by **reducing tacrolimus immunosuppression** + **rituximab** [anti-CD20]) and calcineurin-inhibitor nephrotoxicity.
