## OVERVIEW AND EPIDEMIOLOGY
**Cystic diseases of the biliary tract and liver** comprise congenital dilatation of the extrahepatic and/or intrahepatic biliary tree (**choledochal cysts [Todani Types I–V]**) and fibropolycystic liver diseases resulting from **ductal plate malformations** of the primary cilium (**congenital hepatic fibrosis, Caroli disease, Caroli syndrome, and polycystic liver disease**).

## CLINICAL MANIFESTATIONS AND PATHOPHYSIOLOGY
**Choledochal cysts** (1 in 10,000–15,000 in Western populations, 1 in 1,000 in East Asia; 4:1 female predominance; often associated with an **anomalous pancreaticobiliary ductal union [APBDU]** allowing pancreatic juice reflux into the bile duct) are classified by the **Todani system**: **Type I** (80–90%: fusiform or saccular dilatation of the extrahepatic common bile duct), **Type II** (true diverticulum of the common bile duct), **Type III** (choledochocele within the duodenal wall), **Type IV** (multiple extrahepatic and intrahepatic cysts [IVA] or multiple extrahepatic cysts [IVB]), and **Type V (Caroli disease)**: multifocal segmental saccular ectasia of the large intrahepatic bile ducts. When Type V intrahepatic biliary ectasia is combined with **congenital hepatic fibrosis (CHF)** and autosomal recessive polycystic kidney disease (**ARPKD**, *PKHD1* [263200]), it is termed **Caroli syndrome**. The classic triad of choledochal cyst—**jaundice, right upper quadrant mass, and abdominal pain**—occurs in <20% of children; infants typically present with conjugated hyperbilirubinemia and acholic stools mimicking biliary atresia, while older children present with recurrent cholangitis or pancreatitis.

## DIAGNOSIS AND DIFFERENTIAL EVALUATION
**Abdominal ultrasound** is the initial diagnostic modality of choice (including prenatal ultrasound detection), supplemented by **MRCP (magnetic resonance cholangiopancreatography)** to map the exact Todani type and anomalous pancreaticobiliary junction preoperatively. In Caroli disease/syndrome, CT/MRI demonstrates the pathognomonic **'central dot sign'** (enhancing portal vein radicles surrounded by dilated intrahepatic bile ducts).

| Clinical Entity / Parameter | Pathophysiology, Diagnosis & Management |
| --- | --- |
| **Todani Type I Choledochal Cyst (80–90%)** | Fusiform/cystic dilatation of extrahepatic CBD; associated with anomalous pancreaticobiliary junction; treat with complete cyst excision + Roux-en-Y hepaticojejunostomy |

| **Malignancy Risk in Choledochal Cysts** | High lifetime risk of cholangiocarcinoma if cyst wall is left in situ; internal drainage without excision is contraindicated |

## TREATMENT AND PROGNOSIS
**Choledochal cysts (Types I, II, IVA)** require **complete surgical excision of the cyst mucosa down to the pancreaticobiliary junction with Roux-en-Y hepaticojejunostomy** (never simple internal cystenterostomy drainage!) to eliminate recurrent cholangitis, pancreatitis, and the **10–30% lifetime risk of cholangiocarcinoma** arising in retained cyst wall epithelium. **Congenital hepatic fibrosis** has preserved hepatocyte synthetic function but causes severe **non-cirrhotic presinusoidal portal hypertension** (managed via endoscopic variceal ligation or portosystemic shunting). **Caroli disease/syndrome** complicated by recurrent cholangitis is managed with antibiotics, UDCA, lobar resection (if unilobar), or liver(-kidney) transplantation.
