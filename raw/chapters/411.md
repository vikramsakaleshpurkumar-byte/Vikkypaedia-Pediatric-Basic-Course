# Chapter 411: Drug- and Toxin-Induced Liver Injury
*Frederick J. Suchy and Amy G. Feldman*

## OVERVIEW AND EPIDEMIOLOGY
**Drug- and toxin-induced liver injury (DILI)** in children ranges from asymptomatic transaminase elevations to **pediatric acute liver failure (PALF)** (~15–20% of all PALF cases). DILI is classified mechanistically as **intrinsic (dose-dependent, predictable)**—exemplified by **acetaminophen (paracetamol)**—or **idiosyncratic (dose-independent, unpredictable)**, mediated by metabolic bioactivation or immune hypersensitivity.

## CLINICAL MANIFESTATIONS AND PATHOPHYSIOLOGY
**Intrinsic DILI**: **Acetaminophen overdose** (>150 mg/kg acute single ingestion or repeated supratherapeutic dosing >90 mg/kg/day for >48 hours in febrile/fasting children) saturates hepatic sulfation and glucuronidation, shunting acetaminophen via **CYP2E1** to the hepatotoxic electrophile **NAPQI**, which depletes glutathione and causes **centrilobular (Zone 3) coagulative necrosis** with extreme AST/ALT elevations (often >3,000–10,000 U/L), mild hyperbilirubinemia initially, and severe coagulopathy. **Idiosyncratic pediatric DILI** culprits include **antimicrobials** (**amoxicillin-clavulanate** [cholestatic/mixed injury], **minocycline** [autoimmune-like hepatitis with ANA+ in teenagers treated for acne], **isoniazid**, **trimethoprim-sulfamethoxazole**, macrolides), **antiseizure medications** (**valproic acid** [microvesicular steatosis, hyperammonemia, *POLG*-associated fatal hepatotoxicity], **phenytoin**, **carbamazepine**, **lamotrigine** [often with **DRESS syndrome**: fever, morbilliform rash, facial edema, eosinophilia, lymphadenopathy 2–8 weeks after initiation]), **antineoplastics** (**6-mercaptopurine, methotrexate, L-asparaginase, busulfan/gemtuzumab** [causing **sinusoidal obstruction syndrome / veno-occlusive disease (SOS/VOD)** with tender hepatomegaly, weight gain, ascites, and jaundice]), and **amatoxin** (*Amanita phalloides* mushroom poisoning).

## DIAGNOSIS AND DIFFERENTIAL EVALUATION
The **R-ratio** = `(ALT / ULN) ÷ (Alkaline Phosphatase / ULN)` classifies DILI into **hepatocellular (R ≥ 5)**, **cholestatic (R ≤ 2)**, or **mixed (2 < R < 5)**. **Hy's Law** (hepatocellular DILI with ALT >3× ULN plus total bilirubin >2× ULN without biliary obstruction) predicts a ≥10% risk of acute liver failure or death. Causality is assessed using the **RUCAM (Roussel Uclaf Causality Assessment Method)** scale.

#### **Table 411.1** Key Clinical, Diagnostic, and Therapeutic Summary for Drug- and Toxin-Induced Liver Injury
| Clinical Entity / Parameter | Pathophysiology, Diagnosis & Management |
| --- | --- |
| **Acetaminophen (Intrinsic Zone 3 Necrosis)** | CYP2E1 → NAPQI depletes glutathione; AST/ALT >3,000 U/L; Rumack-Matthew nomogram at ≥4 hr; antidote: N-acetylcysteine (NAC) |
| **Valproic Acid (Mitochondrial Microvesicular Steatosis)** | Inhibits mitochondrial β-oxidation & CPS1; highest risk <2 yr or with POLG variants; antidote: IV L-carnitine (100 mg/kg/day) |
| **Lamotrigine / Carbamazepine / Phenytoin (DRESS)** | Aromatic anticonvulsant hypersensitivity 2–8 wk after start: fever, eosinophilia, facial edema, hepatitis; stop drug + systemic corticosteroids |
| **Minocycline (Autoimmune-Like DILI)** | Adolescents treated for acne developing arthralgias, fever, ANA+, hypergammaglobulinemia, interface hepatitis; resolves after stopping minocycline ± steroids |

## TREATMENT AND PROGNOSIS
Immediate **discontinuation of the offending agent** is paramount. Specific antidotes include: (1) **N-acetylcysteine (NAC)** (IV loading dose 150 mg/kg over 1 hr, then 50 mg/kg over 4 hr, then 100 mg/kg over 16 hr) for **acetaminophen toxicity** (plotted on the **Rumack-Matthew nomogram** at ≥4 hours post-ingestion) as well as non-acetaminophen acute liver failure; (2) **intravenous L-carnitine (100 mg/kg/day, max 6 g/day)** for **valproate-induced hepatotoxicity or hyperammonemia**; (3) **defibrotide** (25 mg/kg/day IV) for post-HSCT **hepatic sinusoidal obstruction syndrome (SOS/VOD)**; and (4) **silibinin, high-dose penicillin G, and NAC** for *Amanita phalloides* poisoning.
