### Question 1 (Clinical Pediatrics Vignette)
A pediatric patient is evaluated for the following clinical and diagnostic profile: Prior neonatal umbilical vein catheterization/omphalitis; splenomegaly + variceal bleed with NORMAL liver synthetic function; 'cavernous transformation' on Doppler US; Meso-Rex bypass. Which of the following conditions or syndromes is the most likely diagnosis?

- **A.** Extrahepatic Portal Vein Obstruction (EHPVO)
- **B.** Intrahepatic Sinusoidal Portal Hypertension
- **C.** Meso-Rex Bypass vs Distal Splenorenal Shunt
- **D.** Acute Variceal Hemorrhage Protocol

<details><summary><b>Show Answer & Rationale</b></summary>

**Correct Answer: A** — Extrahepatic Portal Vein Obstruction (EHPVO)

**Rationale:** **Extrahepatic Portal Vein Obstruction (EHPVO)** is characterized by: Prior neonatal umbilical vein catheterization/omphalitis; splenomegaly + variceal bleed with NORMAL liver synthetic function; 'cavernous transformation' on Doppler US; Meso-Rex bypass.

**Why Other Options Are Incorrect:** **Intrahepatic Sinusoidal Portal Hypertension** is incorrect (characterized by Biliary atresia (#1), AIH, Wilson disease, CFLD; splenomegaly + varices + impaired hepatic synthetic). **Meso-Rex Bypass vs Distal Splenorenal Shunt** is incorrect (characterized by Meso-Rex shunt (SMV to intrahepatic left portal vein) restores physiological portal flow in EHPVO an). **Acute Variceal Hemorrhage Protocol** is incorrect (characterized by Restrictive RBC transfusion (Hb target 7–8 g/dL) + IV Octreotide (1–2 µg/kg bolus, 1–5 µg/kg/hr) + I).

</details>

### Question 3 (Differential Diagnosis (Portal Hypertension and Varices))
During a pediatric consultation regarding **Portal Hypertension and Varices**, which clinical entity or concept is described by the following profile? "n children result from increased portal venous pressure (portal pressure >10 mm Hg or hepatic venous pressure gradient [HVPG] >5 mm Hg, with variceal bleeding risk when HVPG ≥10–12 mm Hg) due to prehepatic, intrahepatic, or posthepatic resistance to portal blood flow."

- **A.** Intrahepatic causes
- **B.** Portal hypertension and varices
- **C.** CHARACTERISTICS (Mpox (Monkeypox))

<details><summary><b>Show Answer & Rationale</b></summary>

**Correct Answer: B** — Portal hypertension and varices

**Rationale:** Portal hypertension and varices in children result from increased portal venous pressure (portal pressure >10 mm Hg or hepatic venous pressure gradient [HVPG] >5 mm Hg, with variceal bleeding risk when HVPG ≥10–12 mm Hg) due to prehepatic, intrahepatic, or posthepatic resistance to portal blood flow.

### Question 4 (Differential Diagnosis (Portal Hypertension and Varices))
During a pediatric consultation regarding **Portal Hypertension and Varices**, which clinical entity or concept is described by the following profile? "ubdivided into presinusoidal (congenital hepatic fibrosis, schistosomiasis), sinusoidal (biliary atresia, autoimmune hepatitis, Wilson disease, alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency, CFLD), and postsinusoidal (sinusoidal obstruction syndrome [SOS/VOD])."

- **A.** Posthepatic causes
- **B.** Intrahepatic causes
- **C.** CONNECTIVE TISSUE DISORDERS
- **D.** INITIAL EVALUATION

<details><summary><b>Show Answer & Rationale</b></summary>

**Correct Answer: B** — Intrahepatic causes

**Rationale:** Intrahepatic causes are subdivided into presinusoidal (congenital hepatic fibrosis, schistosomiasis), sinusoidal (biliary atresia, autoimmune hepatitis, Wilson disease, alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency, CFLD), and postsinusoidal (sinusoidal obstruction syndrome [SOS/VOD]).
