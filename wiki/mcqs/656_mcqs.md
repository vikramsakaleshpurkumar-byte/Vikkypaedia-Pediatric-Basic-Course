### Question 1 (Clinical Pediatrics Vignette)
A pediatric patient is evaluated for the following clinical and diagnostic profile: Acute transverse myelitis (may occur with GBS); Epidural abscess; Tumors; Poliomyelitis; Enteroviral myelitis; Acute flaccid myelitis; Hopkins syndrome; Vascular malformations; Cord infarction; Fibrocartilaginous embolism; Cord compression from tumor. Which of the following conditions or syndromes is the most likely diagnosis?

- **A.** MYOPATHIES (Guillain-Barré Syndrome)
- **B.** SPINAL CORD LESIONS
- **C.** HIV (Guillain-Barré Syndrome)
- **D.** PERIPHERAL NEUROPATHIES

<details><summary><b>Show Answer & Rationale</b></summary>

**Correct Answer: B** — SPINAL CORD LESIONS

**Rationale:** **SPINAL CORD LESIONS** is characterized by: Acute transverse myelitis (may occur with GBS); Epidural abscess; Tumors; Poliomyelitis; Enteroviral myelitis; Acute flaccid myelitis; Hopkins syndrome; Vascular malformations; Cord infarction; Fibrocartilaginous embolism; Cord compression from tumors; Acute disseminated encephalomyelitis; Bickerstaff brainstem encepha.

**Why Other Options Are Incorrect:** **MYOPATHIES (Guillain-Barré Syndrome)** is incorrect (characterized by Periodic paralyses (hypokalemic or hyperkalemic); Dermatomyositis; Critical illness myopathy/polyneu). **HIV (Guillain-Barré Syndrome)** is incorrect (characterized by Diphtheria; West Nile virus; Cytomegalovirus (radiculitis); Leprosy; Lyme disease; Zika virus). **PERIPHERAL NEUROPATHIES** is incorrect (characterized by Toxic; Vincristine; Thalidomide; Glue sniffing; Heavy metal: gold, arsenic, lead, thallium, mercury;).

</details>

### Question 5 (Pediatric Board Mastery (Guillain-Barré Syndrome))
When formulating an evidence-based diagnostic and management plan for **Guillain-Barré Syndrome** (*Part XXVI: Neuromuscular Disorders*), which of the following pediatric clinical statements is **ACCURATE**? (Item 1)

- **A.** First-line management relies on immediate high-dose aspirin therapy in febrile infants.
- **B.** Diagnostic confirmation relies solely on plain skull radiographs without biochemical, microbiologic, or clinical correlation.
- **C.** Inheritance is exclusively holandric (Y-linked) with 100% penetrance restricted to male neonates.
- **D.** GBS may follow administration of vaccines against rabies, influenza, and conjugated meningococcal vaccine, particularly serogroup C.

<details><summary><b>Show Answer & Rationale</b></summary>

**Correct Answer: D** — GBS may follow administration of vaccines against rabies, influenza, and conjugated meningococcal vaccine, particularly serogroup C.

**Rationale:** **Pediatric Clinical Principle (Guillain-Barré Syndrome):** GBS may follow administration of vaccines against rabies, influenza, and conjugated meningococcal vaccine, particularly serogroup C.

</details>
