### Question 5 (Pediatric Board Mastery (Hereditary Predisposition to Thrombosis))
When formulating an evidence-based diagnostic and management plan for **Hereditary Predisposition to Thrombosis** (*Part XIX: Diseases of the Blood*), which of the following pediatric clinical statements is **ACCURATE**? (Item 1)

- **A.** In general, the prothrombotic tendency conferred by these defects is either a result of an increased procoagulant effect (prothrombin gene pathogenic variant, elevated FVIII, hyperhomocysteinemia) or a dec
- **B.** First-line management relies on immediate high-dose aspirin therapy in febrile infants.
- **C.** Clinical manifestations are permanently restricted to late adulthood and never present during infancy, childhood, or adolescence.
- **D.** The condition is an entirely benign physiologic variant that never warrants laboratory or clinical evaluation.

<details><summary><b>Show Answer & Rationale</b></summary>

**Correct Answer: A** — In general, the prothrombotic tendency conferred by these defects is either a result of an increased procoagulant effect (prothrombin gene pathogenic variant, elevated FVIII, hyperhomocysteinemia) or a dec

**Rationale:** **Pediatric Clinical Principle (Hereditary Predisposition to Thrombosis):** In general, the prothrombotic tendency conferred by these defects is either a result of an increased procoagulant effect (prothrombin gene pathogenic variant, elevated FVIII, hyperhomocysteinemia) or a decreased anticoagulant effect (factor V Leiden, deficiency of protein C, protein S, or antithrombin).

</details>
