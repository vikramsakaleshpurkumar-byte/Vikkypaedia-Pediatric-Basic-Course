- **A.** Enteric Duplication Cyst
- **B.** Congenital Transmesenteric Internal Hernia
- **C.** Mesenteric & Omental Lymphatic Cysts
- **D.** Persistent Vitelline (Omphalomesenteric) Band

**Correct Answer: C** — Mesenteric & Omental Lymphatic Cysts

**Rationale:** **Mesenteric & Omental Lymphatic Cysts** is characterized by: Thin-walled multilocular chylous/serous cysts in ileal mesentery or omentum; present as mobile mass or acute volvulus; surgical excision.

**Why Other Options Are Incorrect:** **Enteric Duplication Cyst** is incorrect (characterized by Mesenteric border of ileum (#1); shares muscular wall & blood supply with bowel; ultrasound 'double-). **Congenital Transmesenteric Internal Hernia** is incorrect (characterized by Herniation of small bowel through a defect in the small-bowel mesentery without a hernia sac; high r). **Persistent Vitelline (Omphalomesenteric) Band** is incorrect (characterized by Fibrous band from distal ileum to umbilicus acting as a pivot point for closed-loop small bowel obst).

### Question 5 (Pediatric Board Mastery (Peritoneal Malformations))
When formulating an evidence-based diagnostic and management plan for **Peritoneal Malformations** (*Part XVI: The Digestive System*), which of the following pediatric clinical statements is **ACCURATE**? (Item 1)

- **A.** First-line management relies on immediate high-dose aspirin therapy in febrile infants.
- **B.** Children present either with a mobile, painless, compressible abdominal mass, progressive abdominal distension (pseudoascites from a giant omental cyst), or an acute surgical abdomen due to intestinal volv
- **C.** Systemic tetracyclines and fluoroquinolones are universally required as routine prophylaxis in all healthy newborns.
- **D.** The condition is an entirely benign physiologic variant that never warrants laboratory or clinical evaluation.

<details><summary><b>Show Answer & Rationale</b></summary>

**Correct Answer: B** — Children present either with a mobile, painless, compressible abdominal mass, progressive abdominal distension (pseudoascites from a giant omental cyst), or an acute surgical abdomen due to intestinal volv

**Rationale:** **Pediatric Clinical Principle (Peritoneal Malformations):** Children present either with a mobile, painless, compressible abdominal mass, progressive abdominal distension (pseudoascites from a giant omental cyst), or an acute surgical abdomen due to intestinal volvulus, internal strangulation, intracystic hemorrhage, or cyst rupture.

</details>
