### Question 1 (Clinical Pediatrics Vignette)

- **A.** Minocycline (Autoimmune-Like DILI)
- **B.** Valproic Acid (Mitochondrial Microvesicular Steatosis)
- **C.** Acetaminophen (Intrinsic Zone 3 Necrosis)

**Rationale:** **Acetaminophen (Intrinsic Zone 3 Necrosis)** (associated with **`CYP2E1`**) is characterized by: CYP2E1 → NAPQI depletes glutathione; AST/ALT >3,000 U/L; Rumack-Matthew nomogram at ≥4 hr; antidote: N-acetylcysteine (NAC).

**Why Other Options Are Incorrect:** **Minocycline (Autoimmune-Like DILI)** is incorrect (characterized by Adolescents treated for acne developing arthralgias, fever, ANA+, hypergammaglobulinemia, interface ). **Valproic Acid (Mitochondrial Microvesicular Steatosis)** is incorrect (characterized by Inhibits mitochondrial β-oxidation & CPS1; highest risk <2 yr or with POLG variants; antidote: IV L-). **Lamotrigine / Carbamazepine / Phenytoin (DRESS)** is incorrect (characterized by Aromatic anticonvulsant hypersensitivity 2–8 wk after start: fever, eosinophilia, facial edema, hepa).

**Rationale:** **Pediatric Clinical Principle (Drug- and Toxin-Induced Liver Injury):** DILI is classified mechanistically as intrinsic (dose-dependent, predictable)—exemplified by acetaminophen (paracetamol)—or idiosyncratic (dose-independent, unpredictable), mediated by metabolic bioactivation or immune hypersensitivity.
