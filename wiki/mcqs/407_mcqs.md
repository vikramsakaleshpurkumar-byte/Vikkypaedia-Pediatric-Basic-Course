### Question 1 (Clinical Pediatrics Vignette)
A pediatric patient is evaluated for the following clinical and diagnostic profile: S. aureus (#1 in children & CGD), E. coli, Klebsiella, anaerobes; US/CT-guided percutaneous drainage + 4–6 wk IV/PO antibiotics; screen for CGD with DHR test. Which of the following conditions or syndromes is the most likely diagnosis?

- **A.** Hepatosplenic Candidiasis
- **B.** Pyogenic Liver Abscess (PLA)
- **C.** Amebic Liver Abscess (Entamoeba histolytica)
- **D.** Hepatosplenic Cat-Scratch Disease

<details><summary><b>Show Answer & Rationale</b></summary>

**Correct Answer: B** — Pyogenic Liver Abscess (PLA)

**Rationale:** **Pyogenic Liver Abscess (PLA)** is characterized by: S. aureus (#1 in children & CGD), E. coli, Klebsiella, anaerobes; US/CT-guided percutaneous drainage + 4–6 wk IV/PO antibiotics; screen for CGD with DHR test.

**Why Other Options Are Incorrect:** **Hepatosplenic Candidiasis** is incorrect (characterized by Neutropenic recovery in oncology patients; 'bull's-eye' target microabscesses on CT/US; liposomal am). **Amebic Liver Abscess (Entamoeba histolytica)** is incorrect (characterized by Solitary right-lobe cavity ('anchovy paste'); E. histolytica serum IgG + (>95%); metronidazole/tinid). **Hepatosplenic Cat-Scratch Disease** is incorrect (characterized by Bartonella henselae after kitten exposure; prolonged fever of unknown origin with stellate necrotizi).
