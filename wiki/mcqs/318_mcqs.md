### Question 1 (Clinical Pediatrics Vignette)
A pediatric patient is evaluated for the following clinical and diagnostic profile: Through an unknown mechanism, the presence of the virus kills these periven tricular cells, leading to periventricular calcifications, a radiographic hallmark of this disorder.. Which of the following conditions or syndromes is the most likely diagnosis?

- **A.** Duodenal Obstruction (Intestinal Atresia, Stenosis, and Malrotation)
- **B.** POSSIBLE NONRHEUMATIC DISEASES CAUSING SIMILAR SYMPTOMS
- **C.** AIRWAY INJURY (Bronchiectasis)
- **D.** Lymphocytic Choriomeningitis Virus

<details><summary><b>Show Answer & Rationale</b></summary>

**Correct Answer: D** — Lymphocytic Choriomeningitis Virus

**Rationale:** **Lymphocytic Choriomeningitis Virus** is characterized by: Through an unknown mechanism, the presence of the virus kills these periven tricular cells, leading to periventricular calcifications, a radiographic hallmark of this disorder..

**Why Other Options Are Incorrect:** **Duodenal Obstruction (Intestinal Atresia, Stenosis, and Malrotation)** is incorrect (characterized by Obstruction is typically associated with bowel distention, which is caused by an accumulation of ing). **POSSIBLE NONRHEUMATIC DISEASES CAUSING SIMILAR SYMPTOMS** is incorrect (characterized by Fevers; Systemic JIA, SLE, vasculitis, acute rheumatic; fever, sarcoidosis, MCTD; Malignancies, infe). **AIRWAY INJURY (Bronchiectasis)** is incorrect (characterized by Bronchiolitis obliterans (e.g., postviral, after lung transplantation); Recurrent pneumonitis or pne).
