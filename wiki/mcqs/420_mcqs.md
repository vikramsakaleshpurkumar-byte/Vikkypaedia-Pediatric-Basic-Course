### Question 1 (Clinical Pediatrics Vignette)
A pediatric patient is evaluated for the following clinical and diagnostic profile: Small defect in linea alba between xiphoid and umbilicus; contains preperitoneal fat; pea-sized midline mass; does NOT resolve spontaneously → elective surgical closure. Which of the following conditions or syndromes is the most likely diagnosis?

- **A.** Diastasis Recti (Differential Diagnosis)
- **B.** Umbilical Hernia (Differential Diagnosis)
- **C.** Epigastric Hernia
- **D.** Incisional Hernia (Subchapter 420.1)

<details><summary><b>Show Answer & Rationale</b></summary>

**Correct Answer: C** — Epigastric Hernia

**Rationale:** **Epigastric Hernia** is characterized by: Small defect in linea alba between xiphoid and umbilicus; contains preperitoneal fat; pea-sized midline mass; does NOT resolve spontaneously → elective surgical closure.

**Why Other Options Are Incorrect:** **Diastasis Recti (Differential Diagnosis)** is incorrect (characterized by Benign thinning/widening of linea alba without a discrete fascial defect; bulges only when crying/st). **Umbilical Hernia (Differential Diagnosis)** is incorrect (characterized by Patent umbilical ring; >85–90% close spontaneously by age 4–5 years; observe unless defect >1.5–2 cm). **Incisional Hernia (Subchapter 420.1)** is incorrect (characterized by Fascial dehiscence at prior laparotomy scar (risk factors: wound infection, steroids, ascites); high).
