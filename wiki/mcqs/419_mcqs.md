### Question 1 (Clinical Pediatrics Vignette)
A pediatric patient is evaluated for the following clinical and diagnostic profile: Nephrotic syndrome or cirrhosis; monomicrobial (S. pneumoniae, E. coli, Klebsiella); ascitic PMN ≥250 cells/mm³; treat with IV Ceftriaxone/Cefotaxime (medical, non-surgical). Which of the following conditions or syndromes is the most likely diagnosis?

- **A.** CAPD (Peritoneal Dialysis) Peritonitis
- **B.** Localized Peritoneal Abscess (Subchapter 419.3)
- **C.** Acute Primary Peritonitis / SBP (Subchapter 419.1)
- **D.** Acute Secondary Peritonitis (Subchapter 419.2)

<details><summary><b>Show Answer & Rationale</b></summary>

**Correct Answer: C** — Acute Primary Peritonitis / SBP (Subchapter 419.1)

**Rationale:** **Acute Primary Peritonitis / SBP (Subchapter 419.1)** is characterized by: Nephrotic syndrome or cirrhosis; monomicrobial (S. pneumoniae, E. coli, Klebsiella); ascitic PMN ≥250 cells/mm³; treat with IV Ceftriaxone/Cefotaxime (medical, non-surgical).

**Why Other Options Are Incorrect:** **CAPD (Peritoneal Dialysis) Peritonitis** is incorrect (characterized by Cloudy dialysate effluent with WBC >100/mm³ (>50% PMNs); Staphylococcus epidermidis or S. aureus (#1). **Localized Peritoneal Abscess (Subchapter 419.3)** is incorrect (characterized by Pelvic, interloop, or subphrenic abscess post-appendicitis; spiking fevers & tenesmus; US/CT-guided ). **Acute Secondary Peritonitis (Subchapter 419.2)** is incorrect (characterized by Perforated appendicitis (#1 in children) or neonatal NEC; polymicrobial (E. coli + Bacteroides fragi).

### Question 2 (Clinical Pediatrics Vignette)
A pediatric patient is evaluated for the following clinical and diagnostic profile: Perforated appendicitis (#1 in children) or neonatal NEC; polymicrobial (E. coli + Bacteroides fragilis); free air on imaging; urgent surgical source control + Pip-Tazo or Ceftriaxone/Metronidazole. Which of the following conditions or syndromes is the most likely diagnosis?

- **A.** CAPD (Peritoneal Dialysis) Peritonitis
- **B.** Acute Secondary Peritonitis (Subchapter 419.2)
- **C.** Acute Primary Peritonitis / SBP (Subchapter 419.1)
- **D.** Localized Peritoneal Abscess (Subchapter 419.3)

<details><summary><b>Show Answer & Rationale</b></summary>

**Correct Answer: B** — Acute Secondary Peritonitis (Subchapter 419.2)

**Rationale:** **Acute Secondary Peritonitis (Subchapter 419.2)** is characterized by: Perforated appendicitis (#1 in children) or neonatal NEC; polymicrobial (E. coli + Bacteroides fragilis); free air on imaging; urgent surgical source control + Pip-Tazo or Ceftriaxone/Metronidazole.

**Why Other Options Are Incorrect:** **CAPD (Peritoneal Dialysis) Peritonitis** is incorrect (characterized by Cloudy dialysate effluent with WBC >100/mm³ (>50% PMNs); Staphylococcus epidermidis or S. aureus (#1). **Acute Primary Peritonitis / SBP (Subchapter 419.1)** is incorrect (characterized by Nephrotic syndrome or cirrhosis; monomicrobial (S. pneumoniae, E. coli, Klebsiella); ascitic PMN ≥25). **Localized Peritoneal Abscess (Subchapter 419.3)** is incorrect (characterized by Pelvic, interloop, or subphrenic abscess post-appendicitis; spiking fevers & tenesmus; US/CT-guided ).
