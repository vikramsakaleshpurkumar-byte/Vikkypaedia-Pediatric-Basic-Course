### Question 1 (Clinical Pediatrics Vignette)

- **A.** PROTEINURIA (Nephrotic Syndrome)
- **B.** Nephrotic Syndrome (Typical)
- **C.** TRANSIENT PROTEINURIA
- **D.** Proteinuria With or Without Nephrotic Syndrome

**Correct Answer: C** — TRANSIENT PROTEINURIA

**Rationale:** **TRANSIENT PROTEINURIA** is characterized by: Fever; Exercise; Dehydration; Cold exposure; Congestive heart failure; Seizure; Stress; Recent use of epinephrine.

**Why Other Options Are Incorrect:** **PROTEINURIA (Nephrotic Syndrome)** is incorrect (characterized by Idiopathic (minimal change) nephrotic syndrome; Focal segmental glomerulosclerosis; Secondary causes). **Nephrotic Syndrome (Typical)** is incorrect (characterized by Finnish-type congenital nephrotic syndrome (absence of nephrin); Focal segmental glomerulosclerosis ). **Proteinuria With or Without Nephrotic Syndrome** is incorrect (characterized by Nail–patella syndrome (pathogenic variant in LMX1B transcription; factor); Alport syndrome (pathogen).

**Rationale:** **Fig. 566.2**: Glomerular capillary wall. The three layers of the capil lary wall (glomerular endothelial cell, glomerular basement membrane [GBM], and podocyte) act as the glomerular filtration barrier (GFB), pre venting proteins and large molecules from passing from the capillary lumen into the urinary space. The podocyte cell body lies within the uri nary space, and the cell is attached to the GBM through foot processes. Adjacent foot processes are separated by the filtration slit, bridged by the slit diaphragm. Disruption of the GFB leads the passage of protein across the capillary wall, leading to proteinuria.
