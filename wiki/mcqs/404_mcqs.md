### Question 1 (Clinical Pediatrics Vignette)
A pediatric patient is evaluated for the following clinical and diagnostic profile: Acholic stools, hepatomegaly, high GGT & MMP-7; biopsy shows bile duct proliferation + bile plugs; IOC gold standard; Kasai portoenterostomy <60 days of life. Which of the following conditions or syndromes is the most likely diagnosis?

- **A.** PFIC1 (ATP8B1 / FIC1) & PFIC2 (ABCB11 / BSEP)
- **B.** Alagille Syndrome (JAG1 / NOTCH2, OMIM #118450)
- **C.** Biliary Atresia (Extrahepatic Fibro-Obliterative Cholangiopathy)
- **D.** Alpha-1 Antitrypsin Deficiency (SERPINA1 PiZZ, OMIM #613490)

<details><summary><b>Show Answer & Rationale</b></summary>

**Correct Answer: C** — Biliary Atresia (Extrahepatic Fibro-Obliterative Cholangiopathy)

**Rationale:** **Biliary Atresia (Extrahepatic Fibro-Obliterative Cholangiopathy)** is characterized by: Acholic stools, hepatomegaly, high GGT & MMP-7; biopsy shows bile duct proliferation + bile plugs; IOC gold standard; Kasai portoenterostomy <60 days of life.

**Why Other Options Are Incorrect:** **PFIC1 (ATP8B1 / FIC1) & PFIC2 (ABCB11 / BSEP)** is incorrect (characterized by Severe low/normal-GGT neonatal cholestasis and intractable pruritus; PFIC2 carries high risk of earl). **Alagille Syndrome (JAG1 / NOTCH2, OMIM #118450)** is incorrect (characterized by High GGT cholestasis, bile duct paucity on biopsy, peripheral pulmonary stenosis, butterfly vertebra). **Alpha-1 Antitrypsin Deficiency (SERPINA1 PiZZ, OMIM #613490)** is incorrect (characterized by Neonatal cholestasis with high GGT; PAS-positive, diastase-resistant globules in periportal hepatocy).

</details>

### Question 5 (Pediatric Board Mastery (Cholestasis))
When formulating an evidence-based diagnostic and management plan for **Cholestasis** (*Part XVI: The Digestive System*), which of the following pediatric clinical statements is **ACCURATE**? (Item 1)

- **A.** Percutaneous liver biopsy in BA demonstrates bile ductular proliferation, portal tract edema/fibrosis, and canalicular/ductular bile plugs; the gold standard confirmation is intraoperative cholangiography
- **B.** First-line management relies on immediate high-dose aspirin therapy in febrile infants.
- **C.** Systemic tetracyclines and fluoroquinolones are universally required as routine prophylaxis in all healthy newborns.
- **D.** Inheritance is exclusively holandric (Y-linked) with 100% penetrance restricted to male neonates.

<details><summary><b>Show Answer & Rationale</b></summary>

**Correct Answer: A** — Percutaneous liver biopsy in BA demonstrates bile ductular proliferation, portal tract edema/fibrosis, and canalicular/ductular bile plugs; the gold standard confirmation is intraoperative cholangiography

**Rationale:** **Pediatric Clinical Principle (Cholestasis):** Percutaneous liver biopsy in BA demonstrates bile ductular proliferation, portal tract edema/fibrosis, and canalicular/ductular bile plugs; the gold standard confirmation is intraoperative cholangiography (IOC) showing failure of contrast to pass through the extrahepatic biliary tree.

</details>
