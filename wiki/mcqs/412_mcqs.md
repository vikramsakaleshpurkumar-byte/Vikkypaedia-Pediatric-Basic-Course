### Question 1 (Clinical Pediatrics Vignette)
A pediatric patient is evaluated for the following clinical and diagnostic profile: INR ≥1.5 (PT ≥15 s) WITH hepatic encephalopathy, OR INR ≥2.0 (PT ≥20 s) WITHOUT encephalopathy (after IV Vitamin K administration). Which of the following conditions or syndromes is the most likely diagnosis?

- **A.** Older Child PALF Must-Not-Miss
- **B.** Neonatal PALF (<28 Days) Must-Not-Miss
- **C.** PALF Diagnostic Criteria (Vitamin K–Refractory)
- **D.** PICU Neuroprotection in PALF

<details><summary><b>Show Answer & Rationale</b></summary>

**Correct Answer: C** — PALF Diagnostic Criteria (Vitamin K–Refractory)

**Rationale:** **PALF Diagnostic Criteria (Vitamin K–Refractory)** is characterized by: INR ≥1.5 (PT ≥15 s) WITH hepatic encephalopathy, OR INR ≥2.0 (PT ≥20 s) WITHOUT encephalopathy (after IV Vitamin K administration).

**Why Other Options Are Incorrect:** **Older Child PALF Must-Not-Miss** is incorrect (characterized by Acetaminophen (NAC), Fulminant Wilson Disease (Coombs-neg hemolysis, Alk Phos:Bili 2.2 → liver trans). **Neonatal PALF (<28 Days) Must-Not-Miss** is incorrect (characterized by HSV (empiric IV acyclovir 20 mg/kg q8h!), GALD (IVIG + exchange transfusion), Galactosemia (soy form). **PICU Neuroprotection in PALF** is incorrect (characterized by Continuous D10W–D20W (glucose 90–140 mg/dL), HOB 30°, 3% hypertonic saline (Na 145–155 mEq/L), CRRT ).
