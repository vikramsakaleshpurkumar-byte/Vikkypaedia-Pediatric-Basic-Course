### Question 1 (Clinical Pediatrics Vignette)
A pediatric patient is evaluated for the following clinical and diagnostic profile: Fecal-oral Picornavirus; anti-[condition] IgM diagnostic; 0% chronicity; prevent with universal 2-dose [condition] vaccine starting at 12–23 months. Which of the following conditions or syndromes is the most likely diagnosis?

- **A.** Hepatitis B Virus (HBV) Perinatal Prophylaxis
- **B.** Hepatitis A Virus (HAV)
- **C.** Hepatitis D (HDV) & Hepatitis E (HEV)
- **D.** Hepatitis C Virus (HCV) in Children

<details><summary><b>Show Answer & Rationale</b></summary>

**Correct Answer: B** — Hepatitis A Virus (HAV)

**Rationale:** **Hepatitis A Virus (HAV)** is characterized by: Fecal-oral Picornavirus; anti-HAV IgM diagnostic; 0% chronicity; prevent with universal 2-dose HAV vaccine starting at 12–23 months.

**Why Other Options Are Incorrect:** **Hepatitis B Virus (HBV) Perinatal Prophylaxis** is incorrect (characterized by 90% neonatal chronicity without prophylaxis; give HBV vaccine + HBIG 0.5 mL IM within 12 hr of birth). **Hepatitis D (HDV) & Hepatitis E (HEV)** is incorrect (characterized by HDV requires HBsAg coat (prevented by HBV vaccination); HEV is fecal-oral waterborne virus causing s). **Hepatitis C Virus (HCV) in Children** is incorrect (characterized by Vertical transmission ~5%; check HCV RNA PCR at 2–6 mo or anti-HCV ≥18 mo; cure at ≥3 yr with 8–12 w).

</details>

### Question 3 (Differential Diagnosis (Viral Hepatitis))
During a pediatric consultation regarding **Viral Hepatitis**, which clinical entity or concept is described by the following profile? "from HBsAg+/HBeAg+ mothers; chronicity is inverse to age (90% chronicity in neonates, 25–30% in children 1–5 years, <5% in adolescents/adults)."

- **A.** Hepatitis A Virus (HAV)
- **B.** perinatal/vertical
- **C.** Hepatitis C Virus (HCV) in Children
- **D.** Gianotti-Crosti syndrome

<details><summary><b>Show Answer & Rationale</b></summary>

**Rationale:** perinatal/vertical from HBsAg+/HBeAg+ mothers; chronicity is inverse to age (90% chronicity in neonates, 25–30% in children 1–5 years, <5% in adolescents/adults).

### Question 5 (Pediatric Board Mastery (Viral Hepatitis))
When formulating an evidence-based diagnostic and management plan for **Viral Hepatitis** (*Part XVI: The Digestive System*), which of the following pediatric clinical statements is **ACCURATE**? (Item 1)

- **A.** First-line management relies on immediate high-dose aspirin therapy in febrile infants.
- **B.** HBV serology distinguishes acute infection (HBsAg+, anti-HBc IgM+), chronic infection (HBsAg+ >6 months, anti-HBc IgG+), immune-tolerant phase (HBeAg+, HBV DNA >10^7 IU/mL, normal ALT), immune-active phase
- **C.** The condition is an entirely benign physiologic variant that never warrants laboratory or clinical evaluation.
- **D.** Clinical manifestations are permanently restricted to late adulthood and never present during infancy, childhood, or adolescence.

<details><summary><b>Show Answer & Rationale</b></summary>

**Correct Answer: B** — HBV serology distinguishes acute infection (HBsAg+, anti-HBc IgM+), chronic infection (HBsAg+ >6 months, anti-HBc IgG+), immune-tolerant phase (HBeAg+, HBV DNA >10^7 IU/mL, normal ALT), immune-active phase

**Rationale:** **Pediatric Clinical Principle (Viral Hepatitis):** HBV serology distinguishes acute infection (HBsAg+, anti-HBc IgM+), chronic infection (HBsAg+ >6 months, anti-HBc IgG+), immune-tolerant phase (HBeAg+, HBV DNA >10^7 IU/mL, normal ALT), immune-active phase (elevated ALT, elevated HBV DNA), window period (anti-HBc IgM+ only), and vaccine immunity (anti-HBs+ only).

</details>
