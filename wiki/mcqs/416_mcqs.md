### Question 1 (Clinical Pediatrics Vignette)
A pediatric patient is evaluated for the following clinical and diagnostic profile: 1. Biliary atresia (35–45%), 2. Metabolic/genetic diseases (20%), 3. Pediatric acute liver failure (15%), 4. Unresectable hepatoblastoma (8–10%). Which of the following conditions or syndromes is the most likely diagnosis?

- **A.** Top Pediatric Liver Transplant Indications
- **B.** PELD Score (<12 Years Old)
- **C.** Hepatic Artery Thrombosis (HAT)
- **D.** EBV-Associated PTLD

<details><summary><b>Show Answer & Rationale</b></summary>

**Correct Answer: A** — Top Pediatric Liver Transplant Indications

**Rationale:** **Top Pediatric Liver Transplant Indications** is characterized by: 1. Biliary atresia (35–45%), 2. Metabolic/genetic diseases (20%), 3. Pediatric acute liver failure (15%), 4. Unresectable hepatoblastoma (8–10%).

**Why Other Options Are Incorrect:** **PELD Score (<12 Years Old)** is incorrect (characterized by Incorporates Total Bilirubin, INR, Serum Albumin, Age <1 yr, and Growth Failure (weight/height z-sco). **Hepatic Artery Thrombosis (HAT)** is incorrect (characterized by Most common & severe early vascular complication (<14 days) in small pediatric grafts; causes biliar). **EBV-Associated PTLD** is incorrect (characterized by B-cell lymphoproliferation in EBV-naive pediatric recipients; monitor serial blood EBV PCR; first-li).
