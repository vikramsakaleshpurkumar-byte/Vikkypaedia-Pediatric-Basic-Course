### [Chapter 198: Reactive and Postinfectious Arthritis](../chapters/198.md)
- **Core Clinical Pearl:** Acute rheumatic fever caused by group A streptococcus, arthritis associated with infective endocarditis, and the tenosynovitis associated with Neisseria gonorrhoeae are similar in some respects to reactive arthritis.
- **Key Genes:** `PSTPIP1`
### [Chapter 199: Systemic Lupus Erythematosus](../chapters/199.md)
- **Core Clinical Pearl:** Childhood SLE is rare before 5 years of age and is usually diagnosed in adolescence, with a median age at diagnosis of 11-12 years.
- **Key Genes:** `ADAR1`, `DNASE1`, `DNASE1L3`, `DNASE2`, `IFIH1`, `KRAS`, `NRAS`, `RNASH2`, `SAMHD1`, `TMEM173`, `TREX1`
### [Chapter 200: Juvenile Dermatomyositis](../chapters/200.md)
- **Core Clinical Pearl:** The peak age of onset is 7 years with a second peak of onset in late adulthood (45-64 years); however, adult-onset dermatomyositis appears to be a distinctly separate entity, both in prognosis and etiology.
- **Key Genes:** `MDA5`
### [Chapter 201: Scleroderma and Raynaud Phenomenon](../chapters/201.md)
- **Core Clinical Pearl:** Localized scleroderma (LS) is much more common than sys temic sclerosis (SSc) in children, by a 10:1 ratio, with linear scleroderma being the most common subtype.
### [Chapter 202: Behçet Disease](../chapters/202.md)
- **Core Clinical Pearl:** CLINICAL MANIFESTATIONS AND DIAGNOSIS The course of BD is characterized by exacerbations and remissions.

### [Chapter 203: Sjögren Syndrome](../chapters/203.md)
- **Core Clinical Pearl:** CLINICAL MANIFESTATIONS International classification criteria have been developed for the diagnosis of Sjögren syndrome in adult patients, but these criteria apply poorly to children.
