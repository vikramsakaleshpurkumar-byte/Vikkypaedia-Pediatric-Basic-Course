## Chapter Synthesis & Clinical Highlights
### [Chapter 676: General Considerations and Evaluation of the Ear](../chapters/676.md)
- **Core Clinical Pearl:** The most common cause of hearing loss in children is OM.
### [Chapter 677: Hearing Loss](../chapters/677.md)
- **Core Clinical Pearl:** Conductive hearing loss (CHL) is the most common type of hearing loss in children and occurs when sound transmission is physically impeded in the exter nal and/or middle ear, most commonly by otitis media (OM) with effus
- **Key Genes:** `ACTG1`, `CDH23`, `CHD7`, `CLRN1`, `COL11A2`, `COL4A3`, `COL4A4`, `COL4A5`, `COL9A1`, `DIAPH1`, `EDN3`, `EYA4`
### [Chapter 678: Congenital Malformations of the Ear](../chapters/678.md)
- **Core Clinical Pearl:** An unusually prominent or “lop” ear results from lack of bending of the cartilage that creates the antihelix.
- **Key Genes:** `CHD7`, `EYA1`, `FGFR2`, `PAX3`, `SLC26A4`
### [Chapter 679: External Otitis (Otitis Externa)](../chapters/679.md)
- **Core Clinical Pearl:** External otitis results from chronic irritation and maceration from excessive moisture in the canal.
### [Chapter 680: Otitis Media](../chapters/680.md)
- **Core Clinical Pearl:** Accounting for >10% of pediatric primary care encounters, OM is a leading reason for pedi atric visits and use of antibiotics and figures importantly in the dif ferential diagnosis of fever.
### [Chapter 681: Acute Mastoiditis](../chapters/681.md)
- **Core Clinical Pearl:** Mastoiditis, a suppurative infection of the mastoid air cell system, is the most common infectious complication of acute otitis media (AOM), typically affecting younger children.
### [Chapter 682: The Inner Ear and Diseases of the Bony Labyrinth](../chapters/682.md)
- **Core Clinical Pearl:** The presentation mimics that of chronic otitis media and chronic mastoiditis, and this often leads to a delay in diagnosis.
### [Chapter 683: Traumatic Injuries of the Ear and Temporal Bone](../chapters/683.md)
- **Core Clinical Pearl:** Otosclerosis, an autosomal dominant disease that affects only the temporal bones, causes abnormal bone growth that can result in fixation of the stapes in the oval window, leading to progressive hearing loss.
