# Chapter 412: Acute Hepatic Failure
*Frederick J. Suchy and Amy G. Feldman*

## OVERVIEW AND EPIDEMIOLOGY
**Acute hepatic failure (Pediatric Acute Liver Failure [PALF])** is a rapidly progressive, life-threatening multisystem clinical syndrome in which a child **with no known prior chronic liver disease** develops biochemical evidence of acute liver injury accompanied by uncorrectable **vitamin K–refractory coagulopathy** within 8 weeks of symptom onset.

## CLINICAL MANIFESTATIONS AND PATHOPHYSIOLOGY
By consensus **PALF Study Group criteria**, diagnosis in children requires: (1) no known chronic liver disease, (2) biochemical evidence of acute liver injury, AND (3) parenteral Vitamin K–unresponsive hepatic coagulopathy defined as **PT ≥15 seconds or INR ≥1.5 IN THE PRESENCE of clinical hepatic encephalopathy**, OR **PT ≥20 seconds or INR ≥2.0 REGARDLESS of the presence or absence of clinical hepatic encephalopathy** (because encephalopathy is subtle or late in neonates and infants!). Etiology in high-resource countries is **indeterminate in ~35–45%**, followed by **acetaminophen toxicity (~15%)**, metabolic diseases (~10–15%: Wilson disease, galactosemia, tyrosinemia type 1, mitochondrial *DGUOK*/*POLG*/*TRMU*, fatty acid oxidation defects), viral infections (HSV in neonates, adenovirus 41/AAV2, HAV, HBV, EBV), **GALD (neonatal hemochromatosis)**, autoimmune hepatitis, and HLH.

## DIAGNOSIS AND DIFFERENTIAL EVALUATION
Urgent age-stratified diagnostic workup is performed immediately upon admission to a pediatric liver transplant center: in **neonates (<28 days)**, test immediately for **disseminated HSV (blood/surface HSV PCR — start empiric IV acyclovir 20 mg/kg q8h immediately!)**, **GALD** (ferritin, alpha-fetoprotein, lip biopsy/MRI), **galactosemia** (RBC GALT activity), **tyrosinemia type 1** (urine/blood **succinylacetone**), and mitochondrial *DGUOK*/*TRMU*. In **older children**, check **acetaminophen level**, **ceruloplasmin / 24-hr urine copper / alkaline phosphatase:bilirubin ratio <4 (Wilson disease)**, **ANA/ASMA/anti-LKM-1/IgG (AIH)**, viral PCRs, and soluble IL-2R/ferritin (HLH).

| --- | --- |
| **PALF Diagnostic Criteria (Vitamin K–Refractory)** | INR ≥1.5 (PT ≥15 s) WITH hepatic encephalopathy, OR INR ≥2.0 (PT ≥20 s) WITHOUT encephalopathy (after IV Vitamin K administration) |

| **PICU Neuroprotection in PALF** | Continuous D10W–D20W (glucose 90–140 mg/dL), HOB 30°, 3% hypertonic saline (Na 145–155 mEq/L), CRRT for ammonia >200 µmol/L; do NOT mask INR with routine FFP |

## TREATMENT AND PROGNOSIS
Intensive PICU management in a **liver transplant center** focuses on: (1) **preventing hypoglycemia** via continuous IV dextrose (D10W–D20W to maintain blood glucose 90–140 mg/dL), (2) **neuroprotective management of cerebral edema and hepatic encephalopathy** (head of bed elevated 30°, minimal stimulation, endotracheal intubation for Grade III–IV encephalopathy, **hypertonic 3% saline** to keep serum Na 145–155 mEq/L or **mannitol** for intracranial hypertension, continuous renal replacement therapy [CRRT] for hyperammonemia >150–200 µmol/L), (3) **avoiding routine prophylactic FFP/platelet transfusions** unless active bleeding or an invasive procedure is planned (so INR remains an accurate prognostic trajectory marker!), (4) targeted therapy (**IV acyclovir** for neonatal HSV, **NAC** for acetaminophen, **IVIG + exchange transfusion** for GALD, **nitisinone [NTBC]** for tyrosinemia type 1, **steroids** for AIH), and (5) **emergent living- or deceased-donor liver transplantation**.
