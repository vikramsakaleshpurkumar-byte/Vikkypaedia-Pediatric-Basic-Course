## OVERVIEW AND EPIDEMIOLOGY
**Liver abscess** in children is categorized as **pyogenic liver abscess (PLA)** (~80% of cases in high-resource settings), **amebic liver abscess (ALA)** caused by *Entamoeba histolytica*, or fungal/granulomatous microabscesses (*Candida* spp., *Bartonella henselae* cat-scratch disease).

## CLINICAL MANIFESTATIONS AND PATHOPHYSIOLOGY
Predisposing factors for **pyogenic liver abscess** in children include **chronic granulomatous disease (CGD)** (*CYBB*, *NCF1*; catalase-positive organisms), umbilical vein catheterization in neonates, portal pyemia from perforated appendicitis or inflammatory bowel disease, biliary tract obstruction/ascending cholangitis, sickle cell disease, and penetrating trauma. **Staphylococcus aureus** is the single most common pediatric pathogen (especially in CGD), followed by *Escherichia coli*, *Klebsiella pneumoniae*, anaerobes (*Bacteroides*), and *Streptococcus anginosus* group. Children present with fever, chills, right upper quadrant (RUQ) pain, tender hepatomegaly, right basilar pleural effusion/atelectasis, and leukocytosis with elevated ESR/CRP and alkaline phosphatase. **Amebic liver abscess** typically presents as a solitary right-lobe cavity ('anchovy paste' necrotic fluid) in children who have resided in or traveled to endemic areas, often without concurrent dysentery.

## DIAGNOSIS AND DIFFERENTIAL EVALUATION
**Ultrasound** and **contrast-enhanced CT or MRI** identify solitary or multiple hypodense hepatic lesions with rim enhancement, gas bubbles, or septations (right hepatic lobe involved in ~75%). Blood cultures and **ultrasound/CT-guided diagnostic and therapeutic aspiration** (Gram stain, aerobic/anaerobic/fungal/AFB cultures) establish the microbiologic etiology in PLA. **Entamoeba histolytica serum IgG EIA** is positive in >95% of amebic liver abscesses. Any child with a *S. aureus* or *Serratia* liver abscess without an obvious source must be screened for **CGD via the dihydrorhodamine-123 (DHR) flow cytometry assay**.

#### **Table 407.1** Key Clinical, Diagnostic, and Therapeutic Summary for Liver Abscess
| Clinical Entity / Parameter | Pathophysiology, Diagnosis & Management |
| --- | --- |
| **Pyogenic Liver Abscess (PLA)** | S. aureus (#1 in children & CGD), E. coli, Klebsiella, anaerobes; US/CT-guided percutaneous drainage + 4–6 wk IV/PO antibiotics; screen for CGD with DHR test |
| **Amebic Liver Abscess (Entamoeba histolytica)** | Solitary right-lobe cavity ('anchovy paste'); E. histolytica serum IgG + (>95%); metronidazole/tinidazole PLUS luminal agent (paromomycin or iodoquinol); avoid drainage unless imminent rupture |
| **Hepatosplenic Candidiasis** | Neutropenic recovery in oncology patients; 'bull's-eye' target microabscesses on CT/US; liposomal amphotericin B or echinocandin (micafungin/caspofungin) followed by fluconazole |
| **Hepatosplenic Cat-Scratch Disease** | Bartonella henselae after kitten exposure; prolonged fever of unknown origin with stellate necrotizing granulomas; azithromycin ± rifampin |

## TREATMENT AND PROGNOSIS
**Pyogenic liver abscess** requires **percutaneous needle aspiration or pigtail catheter drainage** (for abscesses >3–5 cm) combined with 4–6 weeks of broad-spectrum intravenous followed by oral antimicrobial therapy (e.g., vancomycin or cefazolin/nafcillin + ceftriaxone/piperacillin-tazobactam + metronidazole, tailored to cultures; corticosteroids are added alongside antibiotics in CGD-associated hyperinflammatory liver abscesses). **Uncomplicated amebic liver abscess** is treated medically **without drainage** using **metronidazole (35–50 mg/kg/day divided TID for 7–10 days)** or tinidazole, **mandatory followed by a luminal amebicide** (**paromomycin** 25–35 mg/kg/day TID for 7 days or **iodoquinol**) to eradicate intestinal cyst carriage.
