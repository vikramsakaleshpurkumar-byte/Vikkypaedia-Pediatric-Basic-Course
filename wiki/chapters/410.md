*Amy G. Feldman and Frederick J. Suchy*

## OVERVIEW AND EPIDEMIOLOGY
**Autoimmune hepatitis (AIH)** is a progressive, T-cell-mediated chronic inflammatory liver disease of childhood and adolescence characterized by female predominance (~75%), **hypergammaglobulinemia (elevated serum IgG)**, circulating non-organ-specific autoantibodies, **interface hepatitis** on liver histology, and a dramatic response to immunosuppressive therapy.

## CLINICAL MANIFESTATIONS AND PATHOPHYSIOLOGY
Pediatric AIH is classified into two major serological subtypes: **Type 1 AIH** (~80% of cases; peaks in peripubertal/adolescent girls, positive for **antinuclear antibodies [ANA]** and/or **anti-smooth muscle antibodies [ASMA / anti-actin]**) and **Type 2 AIH** (~15–20% of pediatric cases; presents in younger children and infants, positive for **anti-liver-kidney microsomal type 1 [anti-LKM-1]** directed against CYP2D6 and/or **anti-liver cytosol type 1 [anti-LC-1]**, frequently accompanied by partial IgA deficiency and a higher rate of acute fulminant presentation). Up to 20–30% of children with AIH have **autoimmune sclerosing cholangitis (ASC / AIH-PSC overlap syndrome)**, often associated with inflammatory bowel disease.

## DIAGNOSIS AND DIFFERENTIAL EVALUATION
Diagnosis requires exclusion of Wilson disease, viral hepatitis, alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency, and drug-induced liver injury, combined with: (1) **elevated total serum IgG** (>1.2–1.5× upper limit of normal), (2) positive autoantibody titers (**ANA, ASMA ≥1:20, or anti-LKM-1 ≥1:10** in children), (3) **percutaneous liver biopsy** showing **interface hepatitis (piece-meal necrosis) with a dense portal lymphoplasmacytic infiltrate**, emperipolesis, and hepatocyte rosettes, and (4) **MRCP** in all pediatric AIH patients to detect concomitant **autoimmune sclerosing cholangitis (ASC)**.

#### **Table 410.1** Key Clinical, Diagnostic, and Therapeutic Summary for Autoimmune Hepatitis
| Clinical Entity / Parameter | Pathophysiology, Diagnosis & Management |
| --- | --- |
| **Autoimmune Hepatitis Type 1 (AIH-1)** | 80% of pediatric AIH; older children/adolescents; ANA+ and/or anti-smooth muscle antibody (ASMA / anti-actin)+; associated with HLA-DRB1*0301 / *0401 |
| **Autoimmune Hepatitis Type 2 (AIH-2)** | Younger children/infants; anti-LKM-1+ (anti-CYP2D6) and/or anti-LC-1+; higher risk of acute liver failure; associated with autoimmune polyendocrinopathy (APECED / AIRE) |
| **Autoimmune Sclerosing Cholangitis (ASC)** | Overlap of AIH + bile duct sclerosis on MRCP in 20–30% of pediatric cases; strongly linked to ulcerative colitis; add ursodeoxycholic acid (UDCA) to immunosuppression |
| **Standard Immunosuppressive Regimen** | Prednisone 1–2 mg/kg/day (max 60 mg) + Azathioprine 1–2 mg/kg/day (verify normal TPMT/NUDT15 first); liver biopsy showing interface plasma-cell hepatitis required |

## TREATMENT AND PROGNOSIS
First-line induction therapy consists of **prednisone or prednisolone (1–2 mg/kg/day, maximum 40–60 mg/day)**, tapered gradually over 6–8 weeks as transaminases normalize, combined with **azathioprine (1–2 mg/kg/day)** as a steroid-sparing maintenance agent (**check TPMT and NUDT15 genotype/activity before starting azathioprine** to prevent life-threatening myelosuppression; delay azathioprine until jaundice resolves in severely icteric patients). **Budesonide** (9 mg/day) + azathioprine is an alternative in non-cirrhotic patients. Second-line salvage therapy for azathioprine intolerance or refractory AIH is **mycophenolate mofetil (MMF)** or **tacrolimus**.
