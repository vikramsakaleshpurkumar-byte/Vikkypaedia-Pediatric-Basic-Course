# Chapter 417: Peritoneal Malformations
*Jamie F. Merves and Chris A. Liacouras*

## OVERVIEW AND EPIDEMIOLOGY
**Peritoneal malformations** and congenital anomalies of the mesentery and omentum arise from incomplete embryologic fusion, canalization, or lymphatic development of the dorsal and ventral mesenteries during fetal midgut rotation and fixation.

## CLINICAL MANIFESTATIONS AND PATHOPHYSIOLOGY
Key anomalies include **mesenteric and omental cysts** (mostly benign **lymphangiomas / lymphatic malformations** located in the small-bowel mesentery, particularly the ileum, or greater omentum), **enteric duplication cysts** (which share a common muscular wall and blood supply with the adjacent bowel and contain heterotopic gastric mucosa in 20–30%), **internal hernias** through congenital mesenteric defects (such as **paraduodenal hernias** or transmesenteric defects), and **persistent omphalomesenteric (vitelline) duct bands** connecting the ileum to the umbilicus. Children present either with a mobile, painless, compressible abdominal mass, progressive abdominal distension (pseudoascites from a giant omental cyst), or an **acute surgical abdomen** due to **intestinal volvulus**, internal strangulation, intracystic hemorrhage, or cyst rupture.

## DIAGNOSIS AND DIFFERENTIAL EVALUATION
**Abdominal ultrasonography** differentiates cystic lymphatic malformations (thin-walled, multilocular septated anechoic cysts) from **enteric duplication cysts** (which display the pathognomonic **'gut signature'**—an inner echogenic mucosal layer surrounded by an outer hypoechoic muscular rim). **Contrast-enhanced CT or MRI** defines retroperitoneal vs intraperitoneal extension and mesenteric vascular involvement.

#### **Table 417.1** Key Clinical, Diagnostic, and Therapeutic Summary for Peritoneal Malformations
| Clinical Entity / Parameter | Pathophysiology, Diagnosis & Management |
| --- | --- |
| **Mesenteric & Omental Lymphatic Cysts** | Thin-walled multilocular chylous/serous cysts in ileal mesentery or omentum; present as mobile mass or acute volvulus; surgical excision |
| **Enteric Duplication Cyst** | Mesenteric border of ileum (#1); shares muscular wall & blood supply with bowel; ultrasound 'double-layered gut signature'; resect with adjacent bowel |
| **Congenital Transmesenteric Internal Hernia** | Herniation of small bowel through a defect in the small-bowel mesentery without a hernia sac; high risk of rapid strangulation and gangrene |
| **Persistent Vitelline (Omphalomesenteric) Band** | Fibrous band from distal ileum to umbilicus acting as a pivot point for closed-loop small bowel obstruction and volvulus |

## TREATMENT AND PROGNOSIS
**Symptomatic or enlarging mesenteric and omental cysts** are treated by **complete surgical enucleation or laparoscopic excision**; when a mesenteric lymphatic malformation infiltrates the bowel wall or mesenteric arcade, segmental bowel resection with primary anastomosis (or image-guided sclerotherapy with doxycycline/bleomycin for unresectable macrocystic retroperitoneal extensions) is required. Congenital transmesenteric defects are closed surgically to prevent lethal midgut/internal herniation.
