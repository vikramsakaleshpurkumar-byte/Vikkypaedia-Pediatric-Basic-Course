# Chapter 409: Mitochondrial Hepatopathies
*Sindhu Pandurangi and William F. Balistreri*

## OVERVIEW AND EPIDEMIOLOGY
**Mitochondrial hepatopathies** are primary or secondary defects of the mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation (OXPHOS) respiratory chain, mitochondrial DNA (mtDNA) maintenance/depletion, or fatty acid β-oxidation that manifest as acute neonatal liver failure, progressive cholestasis, lactic acidosis, or **valproate-triggered fulminant hepatic failure**.

## CLINICAL MANIFESTATIONS AND PATHOPHYSIOLOGY
**Mitochondrial DNA depletion syndromes (MDS)** are autosomal recessive nuclear gene disorders causing severe quantitative reduction of tissue mtDNA content (<20–30% of normal): (1) **Hepatocerebral MDS**: **DGUOK** (deoxyguanosine kinase deficiency; the most common cause of neonatal hepatic mtDNA depletion, presenting in the first weeks of life with neonatal cholestasis, hepatomegaly, hypoglycemia, lactic acidosis, rotatory nystagmus, and hypotonia), **POLG** (**Alpers-Huttenlocher syndrome** [203700]; refractory focal/myoclonic seizures, psychomotor regression, cortical blindness, and fulminant micronodular liver failure often precipitated within weeks of starting **valproic acid**), **MPV17** (Navajo neurohepatopathy), and **TWNK** (Twinkle helicase). (2) **Pearson marrow-pancreas syndrome** (single large-scale mtDNA deletion) presents in infancy with **refractory sideroblastic anemia** (ringed sideroblasts and vacuolated marrow precursors), exocrine pancreatic insufficiency, lactic acidosis, and hepatopathy. Secondary mitochondrial hepatopathies include **Reye syndrome** (acute microvesicular steatosis, hyperammonemia, hypoglycemia, and cerebral edema following **aspirin [salicylate]** use during influenza or varicella).

## DIAGNOSIS AND DIFFERENTIAL EVALUATION
Key biochemical clues include **molar lactate-to-pyruvate ratio >20–25:1** (reflecting an elevated NADH/NAD+ redox state), elevated **3-hydroxybutyrate-to-acetoacetate ratio (>2:1)**, hypoglycemia, hyperammonemia, and elevated plasma tyrosine/alanine. Liver histology shows **microvesicular and macrovesicular steatosis**, oncocytic hepatocyte transformation, and swollen pleomorphic mitochondria on electron microscopy. Definitive diagnosis is established by **next-generation sequencing (whole-exome/genome or mitochondrial gene panel)** for *DGUOK*, *POLG*, *MPV17*, *TWNK*, *TRMU* (**transient infantile liver failure** due to *TRMU* variants, which can recover spontaneously with **L-cysteine + N-acetylcysteine** supplementation!).

#### **Table 409.1** Key Clinical, Diagnostic, and Therapeutic Summary for Mitochondrial Hepatopathies
| Clinical Entity / Parameter | Pathophysiology, Diagnosis & Management |
| --- | --- |
| **Alpers-Huttenlocher Syndrome (POLG, OMIM #203700)** | Intractable epilepsy, developmental regression, cortical blindness + fulminant liver failure triggered by valproic acid; valproate & liver transplant contraindicated |
| **DGUOK Deficiency (Hepatocerebral MDS)** | Most common neonatal mitochondrial liver failure; cholestasis, lactic acidosis (lactate:pyruvate >25), hypoglycemia, nystagmus, high ferritin |
| **TRMU Deficiency (Reversible Infantile Liver Failure)** | Acute infantile liver failure with severe lactic acidosis at 1–6 months; high rate of spontaneous complete recovery when supported with L-cysteine and N-acetylcysteine |
| **Reye Syndrome (Secondary Mitochondrial Injury)** | Post-influenza/varicella + aspirin exposure; vomiting, encephalopathy, cerebral edema, hyperammonemia, microvesicular steatosis without inflammation |

## TREATMENT AND PROGNOSIS
**Valproic acid and salicylates are strictly contraindicated** in any child with suspected mitochondrial disease or unexplained infant epilepsy (*POLG* testing is mandatory before considering valproate). Supportive care includes continuous dextrose infusion to prevent catabolism, correction of lactic acidosis, and cofactor supplementation (coenzyme Q10, riboflavin, thiamine, L-carnitine; **L-cysteine + N-acetylcysteine** for reversible *TRMU* deficiency). **Liver transplantation** is indicated for isolated hepatic *DGUOK* or *MPV17* deficiency **without progressive neurologic involvement**, whereas multisystem progressive encephalomyopathy (e.g., active Alpers-Huttenlocher syndrome) is a contraindication to liver transplantation.
