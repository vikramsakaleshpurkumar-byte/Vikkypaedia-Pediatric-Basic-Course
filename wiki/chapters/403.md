## OVERVIEW AND EPIDEMIOLOGY
**Manifestations of liver disease** and **evaluation of patients with possible liver dysfunction** (Subchapter 403.1) center on distinguishing hepatocellular injury, cholestatic biliary disease, and impaired hepatic synthetic or metabolic function across neonatal, infant, and pediatric age groups.

## CLINICAL MANIFESTATIONS AND PATHOPHYSIOLOGY
Key clinical features include **jaundice** (scleral and cutaneous icterus visible when total serum bilirubin exceeds 2.0–2.5 mg/dL in older children or >5 mg/dL in neonates), **acholic (pale/clay-colored) stools** and dark (tea-colored) urine indicating conjugated hyperbilirubinemia, **hepatomegaly** (normal liver span increases from ~4.5–5 cm at birth to 7–8 cm at age 12 years), **splenomegaly**, **pruritus** (from bile salt/autotaxin accumulation), **spider angiomata**, palmar erythema, xanthomas, **ascites**, caput medusae, and **hepatic encephalopathy** (irritability, sleep reversal, asterixis, coma).

## DIAGNOSIS AND DIFFERENTIAL EVALUATION
Laboratory evaluation stratifies liver disease into three domains: (1) **Hepatocellular injury markers**: **ALT** (alanine aminotransferase, cytosol-specific to liver) and **AST** (aspartate aminotransferase, cytosolic and mitochondrial in liver, muscle, RBCs; AST:ALT >2 suggests Wilson disease or mitochondrial hepatopathy); (2) **Cholestatic / biliary markers**: **conjugated (direct) bilirubin** (pathologic whenever >1.0 mg/dL), **GGT** (gamma-glutamyltransferase; high in biliary atresia, Alagille syndrome, MDR3 [*ABCB4*] deficiency, and sclerosing cholangitis, but **normal/low in PFIC1 [*ATP8B1*], PFIC2 [*ABCB11*], TJP2, MYO5B, and bile acid synthesis defects**), and alkaline phosphatase; and (3) **Hepatic synthetic function**: **prothrombin time (PT) / INR** (half-life of Factor VII is 4–6 hours; Vitamin K 1–10 mg IV corrects coagulopathy from fat-soluble vitamin malabsorption but NOT from hepatocellular synthetic failure), **serum albumin** (half-life ~20 days), **blood glucose**, and **plasma ammonia**.

#### **Table 403.1** Key Clinical, Diagnostic, and Therapeutic Summary for Manifestations of Liver Disease
| Clinical Entity / Parameter | Pathophysiology, Diagnosis & Management |
| --- | --- |
| **Hepatocellular Injury Pattern** | Predominant ALT and AST elevation; check viral hepatitis panel, acetaminophen level, ceruloplasmin (Wilson disease), autoimmune markers (ANA, SMA, LKM-1), CK (rule out myopathy) |
| **High-GGT Cholestasis** | Conjugated bilirubin >1.0 mg/dL + elevated GGT (>100–300 U/L); biliary atresia, Alagille syndrome (JAG1), PFIC3 (ABCB4), alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency, choledochal cyst |
| **Low/Normal-GGT Cholestasis** | Conjugated bilirubin >1.0 mg/dL + GGT <100 U/L; PFIC1 (ATP8B1), PFIC2 (ABCB11), PFIC4 (TJP2), bile acid synthesis defects (HSD3B7, AKR1D1), panhypopituitarism |
| **Synthetic Failure Pattern** | Prolonged PT/INR unresponsive to IV Vitamin K, hypoalbuminemia, hypoglycemia, hyperammonemia; evaluate immediately for pediatric acute liver failure (PALF) |

## TREATMENT AND PROGNOSIS
Diagnostic management prioritizes immediate fractionated bilirubin testing in any infant still jaundiced at ≥2 weeks of age, parenteral Vitamin K1 administration for prolonged INR, nutritional rehabilitation with medium-chain triglyceride (MCT)-enriched formulas (which absorb directly into the portal vein without micellar bile salts), and targeted imaging (Doppler ultrasound, MRCP) or percutaneous liver biopsy.
