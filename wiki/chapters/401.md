# Chapter 401: Pancreatic Tumors
*Meghen B. Browning, Steven L. Werlin, and Michael Wilschanski*

## OVERVIEW AND EPIDEMIOLOGY
**Pancreatic tumors** are rare in infancy, childhood, and adolescence (<0.2% of pediatric malignancies) but encompass distinct histopathologic entities with markedly better surgical outcomes than adult pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma. Key pediatric entities include **solid pseudopapillary neoplasm (SPN / Frantz tumor)**, **pancreatoblastoma**, **pancreatic neuroendocrine tumors (PanNETs)**, and acinar cell carcinoma.

## CLINICAL MANIFESTATIONS AND PATHOPHYSIOLOGY
**Solid pseudopapillary neoplasm (SPN)** (>60% of pediatric pancreatic neoplasms) predominantly affects adolescent females (mean age 13–15 years, female-to-male ratio 9:1) and harbors somatic *CTNNB1* (β-catenin) exon 3 activating mutations; patients present with a slowly enlarging, mildly tender upper abdominal mass. **Pancreatoblastoma** is the classic pancreatic malignancy of early childhood (median age 4–5 years), exhibits characteristic **squamoid corpuscles** on histology, is associated with **Beckwith-Wiedemann syndrome (11p15.5)** and **familial adenomatous polyposis (*APC*)**, and secretes **alpha-fetoprotein (AFP)** in ~65% of cases. Functioning **PanNETs** include **insulinoma** (Whipple triad: fasting/exercise hypoglycemia <50 mg/dL, neuroglycopenic symptoms, relief with glucose; associated with **MEN1** [131100]) and **gastrinoma** (Zollinger-Ellison syndrome: refractory peptic ulcer disease and secretory diarrhea).

## DIAGNOSIS AND DIFFERENTIAL EVALUATION
Abdominal ultrasound and multiphase **contrast-enhanced CT or MRI** define tumor encapsulation, cystic-solid architecture (classic mixed solid-cystic hemorrhage in SPN), vascular encasement, and hepatic metastases. Serum tumor markers include **AFP** (elevated in pancreatoblastoma), chromogranin A, fasting insulin/C-peptide/proinsulin (insulinoma), and fasting gastrin (gastrinoma). DOTATATE PET/CT localizes somatostatin-receptor-positive PanNETs.

#### **Table 401.1** Key Clinical, Diagnostic, and Therapeutic Summary for Pancreatic Tumors
| Clinical Entity / Parameter | Pathophysiology, Diagnosis & Management |
| --- | --- |
| **Solid Pseudopapillary Neoplasm (SPN / Frantz Tumor)** | Adolescent females; somatic CTNNB1 (nuclear β-catenin+, CD10+); mixed solid-cystic encapsulated mass; >95% cure with surgical resection |
| **Pancreatoblastoma** | Children 1–8 yr; Beckwith-Wiedemann & APC associations; elevated serum AFP; histologic squamoid corpuscles; resection + cisplatin/doxorubicin (PLADO) |
| **Insulinoma (PanNET)** | MEN1 (MEN1 gene, 11q13) association; Whipple triad with inappropriate insulin & C-peptide during hypoglycemia; diazoxide/octreotide + surgical enucleation |
| **Gastrinoma (Zollinger-Ellison Syndrome)** | MEN1 association; fasting hypergastrinemia (>1,000 pg/mL), secretin stimulation test+, refractory jejunal ulcers; high-dose PPI + surgical resection |

## TREATMENT AND PROGNOSIS
**Complete surgical resection (R0)** with organ-preserving techniques (central pancreatectomy, spleen-preserving distal pancreatectomy, or pancreaticoduodenectomy [Whipple procedure] for head lesions) is the cornerstone of cure. SPN has >95% long-term survival after complete resection alone. Unresectable or metastatic pancreatoblastoma responds to **PLADO chemotherapy (cisplatin + doxorubicin)** followed by delayed surgical resection.
