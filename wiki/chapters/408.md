## OVERVIEW AND EPIDEMIOLOGY
**Liver disease associated with systemic disorders** encompasses hepatic involvement in obesity/insulin resistance (**Subchapter 408.1: Metabolic Dysfunction–Associated Steatotic Liver Disease [MASLD / NAFLD]**), **cystic fibrosis–associated liver disease (CFLD)**, **celiac disease**, **inflammatory bowel disease (primary sclerosing cholangitis [PSC])**, **sickle cell hepatopathy**, **hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis (HLH)**, Fontan-associated liver disease, and total parenteral nutrition (TPN)–associated cholestasis (**IFALD**).

## CLINICAL MANIFESTATIONS AND PATHOPHYSIOLOGY
**Subchapter 408.1: MASLD (formerly NAFLD)** is the **most common cause of chronic liver disease in children and adolescents** in developed nations (~10% of all children and 35–40% of children with obesity), ranging from isolated hepatic steatosis (>5% hepatocytes with macrovesicular fat) to **metabolic dysfunction–associated steatohepatitis (MASH / NASH)**, advanced fibrosis, and cirrhosis. Pediatric MASH frequently exhibits a distinct **Type 2 (periportal / Zone 1)** histologic pattern in younger children (especially boys) with portal inflammation and fibrosis without ballooning, in contrast to adult Type 1 (centrilobular / Zone 3) steatohepatitis; *PNPLA3* (rs738409 I148M) is the strongest genetic risk modifier. **IFALD (Intestinal Failure–Associated Liver Disease)** develops in short-bowel infants receiving prolonged parenteral nutrition with soybean oil lipid emulsions (phytosterol toxicity).

### 408.1 Nonalcoholic Fatty Liver Disease
In **Nonalcoholic Fatty Liver Disease** (*Sarah H. Orkin and William F. Balistreri*), clinical evaluation integrates age-specific history, targeted physical findings, biochemical markers, and definitive imaging or genetic confirmation as detailed above.

## DIAGNOSIS AND DIFFERENTIAL EVALUATION
NASPGHAN guidelines recommend screening overweight/obese children (BMI ≥85th percentile with risk factors or ≥95th percentile) starting at age **9–11 years** using **serum ALT** (upper limit of normal in children: **22 U/L in girls, 26 U/L in boys**). Persistent ALT >2× ULN (>44 U/L in girls, >52 U/L in boys) for >3 months warrants exclusion of Wilson disease, viral hepatitis, autoimmune hepatitis, alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency, celiac disease, and hypothyroidism. **Vibration-controlled transient elastography (FibroScan)**, MR elastography, and MRI proton density fat fraction (MRI-PDFF) quantify fibrosis and steatosis; liver biopsy remains the gold standard for diagnosing MASH.

| --- | --- |
| **Pediatric MASLD / NAFLD (Subchapter 408.1)** | Most common pediatric chronic liver disease; screen at age 9–11 yr with ALT (ULN: 22 U/L girls, 26 U/L boys); PNPLA3 risk allele; lifestyle modification & fructose elimination |

| **IFALD (Parenteral Nutrition Cholestasis)** | Soybean phytosterol toxicity in short-bowel neonates; conjugated hyperbilirubinemia; treat with fish-oil (ω-3) IV lipid emulsion (Omegaven) or SMOFlipid + enteral advancement |

## TREATMENT AND PROGNOSIS
First-line treatment of **pediatric MASLD/MASH** is **multidisciplinary lifestyle modification** targeting gradual weight loss/BMI z-score reduction, complete elimination of sugar-sweetened beverages (fructose drives de novo lipogenesis), and ≥60 minutes/day of moderate-to-vigorous physical activity; GLP-1 receptor agonists (**semaglutide**, **liraglutide**) are effective in adolescents with severe obesity/type 2 diabetes. **IFALD** is reversed/prevented by cycling parenteral nutrition, advancing enteral feeds, preventing central line infections, and switching from pure soybean lipid emulsions to **fish-oil-based (Omegaven) or composite (SMOFlipid) IV lipid emulsions**.
