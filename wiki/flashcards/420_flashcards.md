### Card 1 (Syndrome & Clinical Profile)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> What are the hallmark clinical features and diagnostic profile of **Epigastric Hernia**?</summary>

**Answer:**
**Category / Inheritance:** The Digestive System **Clinical Synthesis:** Small defect in linea alba between xiphoid and umbilicus; contains preperitoneal fat; pea-sized midline mass; does NOT resolve spontaneously → elective surgical closure

</details>

### Card 2 (Syndrome & Clinical Profile)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> What are the hallmark clinical features and diagnostic profile of **Diastasis Recti (Differential Diagnosis)**?</summary>

**Answer:**
**Category / Inheritance:** The Digestive System **Clinical Synthesis:** Benign thinning/widening of linea alba without a discrete fascial defect; bulges only when crying/straining; 0% incarceration risk; reassurance only (no surgery)

</details>

### Card 3 (Syndrome & Clinical Profile)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> What are the hallmark clinical features and diagnostic profile of **Umbilical Hernia (Differential Diagnosis)**?</summary>

**Answer:**
**Category / Inheritance:** The Digestive System **Clinical Synthesis:** Patent umbilical ring; >85–90% close spontaneously by age 4–5 years; observe unless defect >1.5–2 cm persisting past age 4–5 yr or incarcerated

</details>

### Card 7 (Core Pediatric Concept)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> In the pediatric evaluation of **Epigastric Hernia** (*Epigastric Hernia*), how is **extraperitoneal preperitoneal fat (properitoneal fat)** characterized?</summary>

**Answer:**
extraperitoneal preperitoneal fat (properitoneal fat) rather than bowel or omentum, present as a firm, pea-sized, sometimes tender subcutaneous midline nodule midway between the xiphoid and umbilicus that may be irreducible (incarcerated preperitoneal fat), and DO NOT resolve spontaneously.

</details>
