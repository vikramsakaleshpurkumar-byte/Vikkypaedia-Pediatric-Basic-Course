### Card 2 (Pediatric Therapeutics & Management)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> What is the clinical management or therapeutic approach for **Enterococcus**?</summary>

**Answer:**
**Therapy / Protocol:** Vancomycin **Clinical Application:** These isolates are completely resistant to penicillins, necessitating the combination of a penicillin plus a β-lactamase inhibitor or the use of imipenem or vancomycin.

</details>

### Card 3 (Core Pediatric Concept)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> In the pediatric evaluation of **Enterococcus** (*Enterococcus*), how is **ETIOLOGY** characterized?</summary>

**Answer:**
ETIOLOGY Enterococci are gram-positive, catalase-negative facultative anaerobes that grow in pairs or short chains.

</details>

### Card 4 (Core Pediatric Concept)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> In the pediatric evaluation of **Enterococcus** (*Enterococcus*), how is **EPIDEMIOLOGY** characterized?</summary>

**Answer:**
EPIDEMIOLOGY Enterococci are normal inhabitants of the gastrointestinal tract of humans and organisms throughout the animal kingdom, suggest ing they are highly evolved to occupy this niche.

</details>

### Card 5 (Core Pediatric Concept)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> In the pediatric evaluation of **Enterococcus** (*Enterococcus*), how is **PATHOGENESIS** characterized?</summary>

**Answer:**
PATHOGENESIS Enterococci are not aggressively invasive organisms, usually causing disease only in children with damaged mucosal surfaces or an impaired immune system.

</details>
