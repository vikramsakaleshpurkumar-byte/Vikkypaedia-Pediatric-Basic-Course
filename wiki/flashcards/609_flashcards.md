### Card 4 (Pediatric Therapeutics & Management)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> What is the clinical management or therapeutic approach for **Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia Syndromes**?</summary>

**Answer:**
**Therapy / Protocol:** Growth hormone **Clinical Application:** Although these adenomas most commonly secrete prolactin (60–70%), a few secrete growth hormone (5–10%) or adrenocorticotrophic hormone (5%), and the remainder are nonfunctioning (∼25%).

### Card 6 (Core Pediatric Concept)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> In the pediatric evaluation of **Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia Syndromes** (*Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia Syndromes*), how is **Primary hyperparathyroidism** characterized?</summary>

**Answer:**
Primary hyperparathyroidism caused by a parathyroid adenoma or multigland hyperplasia is the most common manifestation of MEN1, with a lifetime cumulative incidence of 90–95%.

</details>

### Card 7 (Core Pediatric Concept)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> In the pediatric evaluation of **Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia Syndromes** (*Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia Syndromes*), how is **Pituitary adenomas** characterized?</summary>

**Answer:**
Pituitary adenomas are the presenting feature of pediatric MEN1 in about 20% of cases and usually occur after age 10 years, although they have been reported in patients as young as 5 years.

</details>
