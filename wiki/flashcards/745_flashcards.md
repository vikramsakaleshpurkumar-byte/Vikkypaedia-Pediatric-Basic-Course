### Card 3 (Pediatric Genetics & Pathogenesis)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> In **Cutis Laxa**, what clinical phenotype or molecular mechanism is associated with **`ALDH18A1`**?</summary>

**Answer:**
Pathogenic variants in ALDH18A1, which encodes delta-1pyrroline-5-carboxylate synthetase (P5CS), a key enzyme in the synthesis of proline (an abundant amino acid in elastin), can cause autosomal dominant or recessive CL.

</details>

### Card 7 (Core Pediatric Concept)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> In the pediatric evaluation of **Cutis Laxa** (*Cutis Laxa*), how is **autosomal dominant** characterized?</summary>

**Answer:**
autosomal dominant form of CL (ADCL) is typically caused by pathogenic variants in ELN, which encodes elastin, an essential extra cellular matrix protein responsible for maintenance of skin elasticity.

</details>

### Card 8 (Core Pediatric Concept)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> In the pediatric evaluation of **Cutis Laxa** (*Cutis Laxa*), how is **Autosomal recessive** characterized?</summary>

**Answer:**
Autosomal recessive CL is divided into three general subtypes: type 1 (ARCL1), with cardiopulmonary complications, type 2 (ARCL2), with CNS and skeletal anomalies, and type 3 (ARCL3; de Barsy syndrome), which adds ocular findings to the type 2 features.

</details>
