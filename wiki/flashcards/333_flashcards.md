### Card 1 (Syndrome & Clinical Profile)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> What are the hallmark clinical features and diagnostic profile of **ACUTE FORMS (American Trypanosomiasis (Chagas Disease; Trypanosoma cruzi))**?</summary>

**Answer:**
**Category / Inheritance:** Infectious Diseases **Clinical Synthesis:** Vectorial; Endemic countries; • Incubation period: 1-2 wk; • Signs of portal of entry: indurated cutaneous; lesion (chagoma) or palpebral edema; (Romaña sign); • Most cases are mild disease (95–99%) and; unrecognized; • Persistent fever, fatigue, lymphadenopathy,; hepatomegaly, splenomegaly, morbilliform; rash, edema;

### Card 2 (Syndrome & Clinical Profile)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> What are the hallmark clinical features and diagnostic profile of **CHRONIC FORMS (American Trypanosomiasis (Chagas Disease; Trypanosoma cruzi))**?</summary>

**Answer:**
**Category / Inheritance:** Infectious Diseases **Clinical Synthesis:** Indeterminate; Endemic and nonendemic; countries; • Asymptomatic; • Normal chest radiograph and 12-lead ECG; • Serology: detection of IgG; • PCR: low sensitivity

### Card 3 (Syndrome & Clinical Profile)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> What are the hallmark clinical features and diagnostic profile of **DIAGNOSIS (American Trypanosomiasis (Chagas Disease; Trypanosoma cruzi))**?</summary>

**Answer:**
**Category / Inheritance:** Infectious Diseases **Clinical Synthesis:** Cardiac and; gastrointestinal; Endemic and nonendemic; countries; • Cardiac manifestations: fatigue, syncope,; palpitations, dizziness, stroke; late; manifestations: chest pain (atypical),; dyspnea, edema, left ventricular dysfunction,; congestive heart failure; alterations in; 12-lead ECG, echocardiography, or other;

</details>

### Card 4 (Pediatric Therapeutics & Management)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> What is the clinical management or therapeutic approach for **American Trypanosomiasis (Chagas Disease; Trypanosoma cruzi)**?</summary>

**Answer:**
**Therapy / Protocol:** Treatment of choice **Clinical Application:** Stage 2 Treatment Combination eflornithine and nifurtimox (NECT) is the current treatment of choice for T. brucei gambiense CNS infection.

</details>
