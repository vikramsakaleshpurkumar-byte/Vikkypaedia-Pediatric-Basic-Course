### Card 1 (Syndrome & Clinical Profile)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> What are the hallmark clinical features and diagnostic profile of **CONGENITAL (Disorders of Vision)**?</summary>

**Answer:**
**Category / Inheritance:** Disorders of the Eye **Clinical Synthesis:** Optic nerve hypoplasia or aplasia; Holoprosencephaly; Septo-optic dysplasia; Optic coloboma; Congenital hydrocephalus; Hydranencephaly; Porencephaly; Microcephaly; Encephalocele, particularly occipital; Morning glory disc; Aniridia; Microphthalmia/anophthalmia syndromes; Persistent pupillary membrane; Cataracts; Persis

</details>

**Answer:**

### Card 6 (Core Pediatric Concept)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> In the pediatric evaluation of **Disorders of Vision** (*Disorders of Vision*), how is **AMBLYOPIA** characterized?</summary>

**Answer:**
AMBLYOPIA This is a decrease in visual acuity, unilateral or bilateral, that occurs in visually immature children as a result of a lack of a clear image pro jecting onto the retina.

</details>

### Card 7 (Core Pediatric Concept)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> In the pediatric evaluation of **Disorders of Vision** (*Disorders of Vision*), how is **diagnosis** characterized?</summary>

**Answer:**
diagnosis of amblyopia is confirmed when a complete ophthal mologic examination reveals reduced acuity that is unexplained by an organic abnormality.

</details>

### Card 8 (Core Pediatric Concept)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> In the pediatric evaluation of **Disorders of Vision** (*Disorders of Vision*), how is **treatment** characterized?</summary>

**Answer:**
treatment first consists of removing any media opacity or prescribing appropriate glasses, if needed, so that a well-focused retinal image can be produced in each eye.

</details>
