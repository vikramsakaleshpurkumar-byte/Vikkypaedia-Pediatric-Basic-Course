### Card 1 (Syndrome & Clinical Profile)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> What are the hallmark clinical features and diagnostic profile of **CLINICAL SYMPTOMS (Sjögren Syndrome)**?</summary>

**Answer:**
**Category / Inheritance:** Rheumatic Diseases of Childh **Clinical Synthesis:** 1. Recurrent parotitis or parotid enlargement; 2. Recurrent conjunctivitis (nonallergic and noninfectious); 3. Recurrent vaginitis; 4. Systemic: fever of unknown origin, arthralgias, hypokalemic; paralysis, or abdominal pain

</details>

**Answer:**
**Category / Inheritance:** Rheumatic Diseases of Childh **Clinical Synthesis:** 5. Ocular dryness (ocular staining or a Schirmer test); 6. Abnormal sialography; 7. Elevated serum amylase; 8. Leukopenia or elevated ESR; 9. Hyperimmunoglobulinemia (polyclonal); 10. Renal tubular acidosis

</details>

### Card 5 (Core Pediatric Concept)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> In the pediatric evaluation of **Sjögren Syndrome** (*Sjögren Syndrome*), how is **EPIDEMIOLOGY** characterized?</summary>

**Answer:**
EPIDEMIOLOGY Sjögren syndrome typically manifests at 35-45 years of age, with 90% of cases among females, but it is underrecognized in children because symptoms often start in childhood.

</details>

### Card 6 (Core Pediatric Concept)

**Answer:**
ETIOLOGY AND PATHOGENESIS The etiology of Sjögren syndrome is complex and includes genetic predisposition and possibly an infectious trigger.

</details>
