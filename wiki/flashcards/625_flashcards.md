### Card 1 (Syndrome & Clinical Profile)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> What are the hallmark clinical features and diagnostic profile of **Gynecomastia**?</summary>

**Answer:**
**Category / Inheritance:** The Endocrine System **Clinical Synthesis:** Some cases may be caused by an increased sensitivity to estrogens and/or relative androgen resistance in the affected tissue.

</details>

### Card 2 (Pediatric Therapeutics & Management)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> What is the clinical management or therapeutic approach for **Gynecomastia**?</summary>

**Answer:**
**Therapy / Protocol:** Insulin **Clinical Application:** Other hormones, including leptin, insulin-like growth factor 1 (IGF-1), and luteinizing hormone, may directly stimulate breast development and may play a role in pubertal gynecomastia.

</details>

### Card 3 (Core Pediatric Concept)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> In the pediatric evaluation of **Gynecomastia** (*Gynecomastia*), how is **PHYSIOLOGIC FORMS OF GYNECOMASTIA** characterized?</summary>

**Answer:**
PHYSIOLOGIC FORMS OF GYNECOMASTIA Gynecomastia occurs in many newborn males as a result of normal stimulation by maternal estrogen; the effect usually disappears in a few weeks.

</details>

### Card 4 (Core Pediatric Concept)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> In the pediatric evaluation of **Gynecomastia** (*Gynecomastia*), how is **Neonatal Gynecomastia** characterized?</summary>

**Answer:**
Neonatal Gynecomastia Transient gynecomastia occurs in 60–90% of male newborns secondary to exposure to estrogens during pregnancy.

</details>

### Card 5 (Core Pediatric Concept)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> In the pediatric evaluation of **Gynecomastia** (*Gynecomastia*), how is **Pubertal Gynecomastia** characterized?</summary>

**Answer:**
Pubertal Gynecomastia During early to mid-puberty, up to 70% of males develop various degrees of subareolar hyperplasia of the breasts.

</details>
