### Card 1 (Syndrome & Clinical Profile)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> What are the hallmark clinical features and diagnostic profile of **Postneonatal Vitamin K Deficiency**?</summary>

**Answer:**
**Category / Inheritance:** Diseases of the Blood **Clinical Synthesis:** Intestinal malabsorption of fats may accompany cystic fibrosis, biliary atresia, or other liver diseases and results in a deficiency of fatsoluble dietary vitamins including vitamin K.

</details>

### Card 2 (Pediatric Therapeutics & Management)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> What is the clinical management or therapeutic approach for **Postneonatal Vitamin K Deficiency**?</summary>

**Answer:**
**Therapy / Protocol:** Factor viii **Clinical Application:** Patients with severe liver disease characteristically have normal to increased (not reduced) levels of factor VIII activity in plasma.

</details>

### Card 3 (Core Pediatric Concept)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> In the pediatric evaluation of **Postneonatal Vitamin K Deficiency** (*Postneonatal Vitamin K Deficiency*), how is **Rat poison** characterized?</summary>

**Answer:**
Rat poison (superwarfarin) produces a similar deficiency that should be considered in young children presenting with bleeding and bruising with a history compatible with ingestion.
