### Card 2 (Syndrome & Clinical Profile)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> What are the hallmark clinical features and diagnostic profile of **Pancreatic Pseudocyst**?</summary>

**Answer:**
**Category / Inheritance:** The Digestive System **Clinical Synthesis:** ≥4 weeks; well-circumscribed encapsulated fluid collection rich in amylase/lipase without solid necrosis; EUS-guided cystogastrostomy if symptomatic

</details>

### Card 3 (Syndrome & Clinical Profile)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> What are the hallmark clinical features and diagnostic profile of **Acute Necrotic Collection (ANC)**?</summary>

**Answer:**

### Card 5 (Core Pediatric Concept)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> In the pediatric evaluation of **Pancreatic Fluid Collections** (*Pancreatic Fluid Collections*), how is **Pancreatic fluid collections (PFCs)** characterized?</summary>

**Answer:**
Pancreatic fluid collections (PFCs) in children develop secondary to acute pancreatitis, pancreatic trauma (especially handlebar bicycle injuries compressing the pancreatic body against the vertebral column), ductal disruption, or hereditary pancreatitis (PRSS1, SPINK1, CFTR, CTRC variants).

</details>

### Card 6 (Core Pediatric Concept)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> In the pediatric evaluation of **Pancreatic Fluid Collections** (*Pancreatic Fluid Collections*), how is **Pancreatic pseudocysts** characterized?</summary>

**Answer:**
Pancreatic pseudocysts mature after ≥4 weeks as encapsulated, enzyme-rich fluid collections bounded by fibrous and granulation tissue without an epithelial lining.

</details>
