### Card 1 (Syndrome & Clinical Profile)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> What are the hallmark clinical features and diagnostic profile of **Mesenteric & Omental Lymphatic Cysts**?</summary>

**Answer:**
**Category / Inheritance:** The Digestive System **Clinical Synthesis:** Thin-walled multilocular chylous/serous cysts in ileal mesentery or omentum; present as mobile mass or acute volvulus; surgical excision

</details>

### Card 2 (Syndrome & Clinical Profile)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> What are the hallmark clinical features and diagnostic profile of **Enteric Duplication Cyst**?</summary>

**Answer:**
**Category / Inheritance:** The Digestive System **Clinical Synthesis:** Mesenteric border of ileum (#1); shares muscular wall & blood supply with bowel; ultrasound 'double-layered gut signature'; resect with adjacent bowel

</details>

### Card 3 (Syndrome & Clinical Profile)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> What are the hallmark clinical features and diagnostic profile of **Congenital Transmesenteric Internal Hernia**?</summary>

**Answer:**
**Category / Inheritance:** The Digestive System **Clinical Synthesis:** Herniation of small bowel through a defect in the small-bowel mesentery without a hernia sac; high risk of rapid strangulation and gangrene

</details>

### Card 4 (Pediatric Therapeutics & Management)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> What is the clinical management or therapeutic approach for **Peritoneal Malformations**?</summary>

**Answer:**
**Therapy / Protocol:** Pediatric Clinical Management **Clinical Application:** Children present either with a mobile, painless, compressible abdominal mass, progressive abdominal distension (pseudoascites from a giant omental cyst), or an acute surgical abdomen due to intestinal volvulus, internal strangulation, intracystic hemorrhage, or cyst rupture.

</details>

### Card 5 (Core Pediatric Concept)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> In the pediatric evaluation of **Peritoneal Malformations** (*Peritoneal Malformations*), how is **Peritoneal malformations** characterized?</summary>

**Answer:**
Peritoneal malformations and congenital anomalies of the mesentery and omentum arise from incomplete embryologic fusion, canalization, or lymphatic development of the dorsal and ventral mesenteries during fetal midgut rotation and fixation.

</details>
