### Card 3 (Pediatric Genetics & Pathogenesis)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> In **Thyroiditis**, what clinical phenotype or molecular mechanism is associated with **`TPO`**?</summary>

**Answer:**
Circulating antibodies to thyroperoxidase (TPO-Abs) or thyroglobulin (Tg-Abs) are detectable in most children with autoimmune thyroiditis and many patients with Graves disease.

</details>

### Card 4 (Pediatric Therapeutics & Management)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> What is the clinical management or therapeutic approach for **Thyroiditis**?</summary>

**Answer:**
**Therapy / Protocol:** Prednisone **Clinical Application:** Nonsteroidal antiinflammatory drugs (NSAIDs) usually alleviate thyroid pain effectively, but a short course of steroids (prednisone) can be considered if pain is severe.

### Card 6 (Core Pediatric Concept)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> In the pediatric evaluation of **Thyroiditis** (*Thyroiditis*), how is **Subacute thyroiditis** characterized?</summary>

**Answer:**
Subacute thyroiditis (de Quervain disease, subacute granulomatous thyroiditis) is believed to have a viral or postviral etiology and is usually transient.

**Answer:**

### Card 8 (High-Yield Board Pearl)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> What is a high-yield clinical or diagnostic principle regarding **Thyroiditis** (*Thyroiditis*)?</summary>

**Answer:**
The thyrotoxicosis caused by thyroiditis is not due to increased thyroid hormone synthesis (in contrast to Graves disease), but rather release of preformed thyroid hormone from the damaged gland, which can last up to 60 days.

</details>
