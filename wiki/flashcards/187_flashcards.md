### Card 2 (Syndrome & Clinical Profile)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> What are the hallmark clinical features and diagnostic profile of **Insect Allergy**?</summary>

**Answer:**
**Associated Gene(s):** `FLG` · **Category / Inheritance:** Allergic Disorders **Clinical Synthesis:** It is associated with generalized redness, scaling, weeping, crusting, systemic toxicity, lymphadenopathy, and fever and is usually caused by superinfection (e.g., with toxin-producing S. aureus or HSV infection) or inappropriate therapy.

</details>

### Card 3 (Pediatric Genetics & Pathogenesis)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> In **Insect Allergy**, what clinical phenotype or molecular mechanism is associated with **`FLG`**?</summary>

**Answer:**
Predictive factors of a poor prognosis for AD include widespread AD in childhood, FLG null pathogenic variants, concomitant allergic rhinitis and asthma, family history of AD in parents or siblings, early age at onset of AD, being an only child, and very high serum IgE levels.

</details>

### Card 4 (Pediatric Therapeutics & Management)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> What is the clinical management or therapeutic approach for **Insect Allergy**?</summary>

**Answer:**
**Therapy / Protocol:** Epinephrine **Clinical Application:** Anaphylactic reactions after a Hymenoptera sting are treated the same as anaphylaxis from any cause; epinephrine is the drug of choice.

</details>
