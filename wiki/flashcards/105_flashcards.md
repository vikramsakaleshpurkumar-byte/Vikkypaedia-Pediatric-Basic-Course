### Card 4 (Pediatric Genetics & Pathogenesis)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> In **Defects in Metabolism of Amino Acids**, what clinical phenotype or molecular mechanism is associated with **`FAH`**?</summary>

**Answer:**
Hereditary causes of hypertyrosinemia include deficiencies of the enzymes fumarylacetoacetate hydrolase (FAH), tyrosine aminotrans ferase (TAT), and 4-hydroxyphenylpyruvate dioxygenase (4-HPPD).

### Card 5 (Pediatric Therapeutics & Management)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> What is the clinical management or therapeutic approach for **609312/DBH**?</summary>

**Answer:**
**Therapy / Protocol:** Neurotransmitter Disorders Affecting Biogenic Amines and GABA Metabolism and Transport: Bi **Clinical Application:** Urine/plasma: ↓↓ norepinephrine and epinephrine; ↑ dopamine and DOPAC; DAT1 deficiency; CSF: ↑ HVA and HVA/5-HIAA ratio

### Card 6 (Pediatric Therapeutics & Management)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> What is the clinical management or therapeutic approach for **Defects in Metabolism of Amino Acids (105.1 Phenylalanine)**?</summary>

**Answer:**
**Therapy / Protocol:** Epinephrine **Clinical Application:** Phenylalanine hydroxylase converts phenylalanine to tyrosine, which is necessary for the production of neurotransmitters such as epinephrine, norepinephrine, and dopamine.

</details>

### Card 7 (Core Pediatric Concept)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> In the pediatric evaluation of **Defects in Metabolism of Amino Acids** (*105.1 Phenylalanine*), how is **phenylalanine hydroxylase (PAH)** characterized?</summary>

**Answer:**
phenylalanine hydroxylase (PAH) or one of its cofactors tetrahydrobiopterin (BH4) causes accumulation of phenylalanine and its metabolites in body fluids and in the brain.

</details>

### Card 8 (Core Pediatric Concept)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> In the pediatric evaluation of **Defects in Metabolism of Amino Acids** (*105.1 Phenylalanine*), how is **severe PAH deficiency** characterized?</summary>

**Answer:**
severe PAH deficiency (also referred to as classical phenylketonuria), plasma phenylalanine levels on an unre stricted diet usually exceed 20 mg/dL (>1,200 μmol/L).

</details>
