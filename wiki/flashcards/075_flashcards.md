### Card 2 (Syndrome & Clinical Profile)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> What are the hallmark clinical features and diagnostic profile of **or adult): Tachycardia; little or no urine output; irritable/lethargic**?</summary>

**Answer:**
**Category / Inheritance:** Fluid and Electrolyte Disord **Clinical Synthesis:** sunken eyes and fontanel; decreased tears; dry mucous; membranes; mild delay in elasticity (skin turgor); delayed capillary; refill (>1.5 sec); cool and pale

</details>

### Card 3 (Syndrome & Clinical Profile)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> What are the hallmark clinical features and diagnostic profile of **adult): Peripheral pulses either rapid and weak or absent; decreased**?</summary>

**Answer:**
**Category / Inheritance:** Fluid and Electrolyte Disord **Clinical Synthesis:** blood pressure; no urine output; very sunken eyes and fontanel; no; tears; parched mucous membranes; delayed elasticity (poor skin; turgor); very delayed capillary refill (>3 sec); cold and mottled; limp,; depressed consciousness

</details>

### Card 6 (Core Pediatric Concept)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> In the pediatric evaluation of **Deficit Therapy** (*Deficit Therapy*), how is **Hypernatremic dehydration** characterized?</summary>

**Answer:**
Hypernatremic dehydration is likely in any child with losses of hypotonic fluid and poor water intake, as may occur with diarrhea, and poor oral intake because of anorexia or emesis.

</details>

### Card 7 (Core Pediatric Concept)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> In the pediatric evaluation of **Deficit Therapy** (*Deficit Therapy*), how is **Hyponatremic dehydration** characterized?</summary>

**Answer:**
Hyponatremic dehydration occurs in the child with diarrhea who is taking in large quantities of low-salt fluid, such as water or formula.

</details>
