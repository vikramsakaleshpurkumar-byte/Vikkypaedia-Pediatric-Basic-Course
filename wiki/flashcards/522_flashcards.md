### Card 1 (Syndrome & Clinical Profile)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> What are the hallmark clinical features and diagnostic profile of **Plasma Transfusions**?</summary>

**Answer:**
**Category / Inheritance:** Diseases of the Blood **Clinical Synthesis:** This includes plasma exchange in a patient with overt bleeding caused by the underlying disorder (e.g., Goodpasture syndrome, vasculi tis) or disorders with significant severe coagulopathy that would substan tially worsen with replacement by albumin solutions only.

### Card 2 (Pediatric Therapeutics & Management)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> What is the clinical management or therapeutic approach for **Plasma Transfusions**?</summary>

**Answer:**
**Therapy / Protocol:** Desmopressin **Clinical Application:** Moreover, mild to moderate hemophilia A and certain types of von Willebrand disease can be treated with intranasal or intravenous desmopressin.

</details>

### Card 3 (Core Pediatric Concept)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> In the pediatric evaluation of **Plasma Transfusions** (*Plasma Transfusions*), how is **PLASMA PRODUCTS AND PATIENT TESTING** characterized?</summary>

**Answer:**
PLASMA PRODUCTS AND PATIENT TESTING Two interchangeable plasma products are available for transfusion, plasma frozen within 8 hours of collection (fresh-frozen plasma [FFP]) and plasma frozen within 24 hours of collection (F24).

</details>

### Card 4 (Core Pediatric Concept)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> In the pediatric evaluation of **Plasma Transfusions** (*Plasma Transfusions*), how is **PLASMA TRANSFUSION IN CHILDREN** characterized?</summary>

**Answer:**
PLASMA TRANSFUSION IN CHILDREN Transfusion of plasma is not recommended for the treatment of patients with severe hemophilia A or B, von Willebrand disease, or factor VII defi ciency because safer plasma-derived and recombinant factor products for VII, VIII, IX, and von Willebrand factor are available.

</details>
