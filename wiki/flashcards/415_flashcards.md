### Card 5 (Core Pediatric Concept)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> In the pediatric evaluation of **Portal Hypertension and Varices** (*Portal Hypertension and Varices*), how is **Portal hypertension and varices** characterized?</summary>

**Answer:**
Portal hypertension and varices in children result from increased portal venous pressure (portal pressure >10 mm Hg or hepatic venous pressure gradient [HVPG] >5 mm Hg, with variceal bleeding risk when HVPG ≥10–12 mm Hg) due to prehepatic, intrahepatic, or posthepatic resistance to portal blood flow.

</details>

### Card 6 (Core Pediatric Concept)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> In the pediatric evaluation of **Portal Hypertension and Varices** (*Portal Hypertension and Varices*), how is **Intrahepatic causes** characterized?</summary>

**Answer:**
Intrahepatic causes are subdivided into presinusoidal (congenital hepatic fibrosis, schistosomiasis), sinusoidal (biliary atresia, autoimmune hepatitis, Wilson disease, alpha-1 antitrypsin deficiency, CFLD), and postsinusoidal (sinusoidal obstruction syndrome [SOS/VOD]).

</details>

### Card 7 (Core Pediatric Concept)
<details><summary><b>Q:</b> In the pediatric evaluation of **Portal Hypertension and Varices** (*Portal Hypertension and Varices*), how is **Posthepatic causes** characterized?</summary>

**Answer:**
Posthepatic causes include Budd-Chiari syndrome (hepatic vein thrombosis) and constrictive pericarditis / post-Fontan physiology.

</details>
